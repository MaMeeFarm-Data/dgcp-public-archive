### DGCP™ Topic #2621: Transport–Trade Dependencies

Transport–trade dependencies connect the movement of goods with production, exchange, distribution, and market systems.

Trade requires physical pathways capable of moving commodities, materials, components, equipment, and finished goods between geographic locations.

Maritime, road, rail, air, inland waterway, pipeline, and intermodal systems therefore form physical layers beneath domestic and international trade.

Bulk commodities, containers, fuels, vehicles, equipment, and other traded goods move through maritime transport networks.

Trade between separated coastal markets therefore connects production and consumption systems with maritime infrastructure and operating capacity.

Ports provide interfaces between vessels and inland transport networks through terminals, cranes, storage areas, roads, railways, pipelines, and logistics systems.

International trade moving by sea therefore interacts directly with port infrastructure before connecting with inland production and distribution networks.
