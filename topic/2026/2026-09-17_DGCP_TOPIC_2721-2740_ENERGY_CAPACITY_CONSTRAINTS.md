### DGCP™ Topic #2721: Energy Capacity Constraints

Energy capacity constraints occur where the physical capacity of energy systems bounds the amount of usable energy that can be produced, converted, transported, stored, or delivered.

Energy capacity exists across extraction, generation, processing, transmission, distribution, storage, fuel transport, and supporting infrastructure.

Available energy resources therefore remain distinct from the operational capacity required to transform and deliver those resources to users.

Energy capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

### DGCP™ Topic #2722: Primary Energy Extraction Capacity

Primary energy extraction capacity connects physical energy resources with the systems capable of bringing those resources into productive use.

Wells, mines, drilling systems, extraction equipment, labor, electricity, water, transport, and supporting infrastructure determine physical extraction capability.

The existence of an energy resource therefore does not independently define the volume that can enter downstream energy systems.

Primary energy extraction capacity remains structurally observable.

Processing facilities can separate, treat, convert, purify, or prepare fuels for transportation, storage, industrial use, or electricity generation.

Energy supply therefore depends on processing capacity located between resource extraction and downstream consumption.

Additional capacity at one stage does not independently determine the usable capacity of the complete energy system.

Energy flows therefore pass through chains of physical capacity distributed across resources, facilities, networks, equipment, and geographic locations.
