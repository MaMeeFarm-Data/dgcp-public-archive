### DGCP™ Topic #2881: Institutional Capacity

Institutional capacity connects formal organizations, administrative structures, rules, information, personnel, and operating systems with the activities they are able to process and coordinate.

Governments, regulators, public agencies, standards bodies, financial institutions, research organizations, and other institutions perform different functions within wider systems.

Physical and economic capacity therefore interacts with institutional structures capable of processing decisions, information, permissions, coordination, and implementation.

Institutional capacity remains structurally observable.

Administrative capacity connects institutional responsibilities with personnel, procedures, records, information systems, budgets, and organizational structures.

Administrative systems process applications, documentation, reporting, coordination, implementation, and other recurring functions.

### DGCP™ Topic #2899: Multi-Layer Institutional Capacity

Multi-layer institutional capacity occurs where system activity depends simultaneously on administrative, regulatory, permitting, licensing, inspection, certification, procurement, data, technical, planning, implementation, monitoring, and coordination functions.

Different institutional layers can operate within separate organizations while remaining connected through a common activity or system.

Institutional capacity therefore emerges from interacting organizational functions rather than formal authority alone.
