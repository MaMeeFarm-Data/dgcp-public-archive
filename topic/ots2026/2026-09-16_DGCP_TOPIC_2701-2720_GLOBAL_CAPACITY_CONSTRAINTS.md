### DGCP™ Topic #2701: Global Capacity Constraints

Global capacity constraints occur where available system capacity limits the volume, speed, scale, or geographic distribution of activity that can be supported.

Capacity exists across resources, energy, infrastructure, industry, transport, logistics, computing, labor, institutions, and other operating systems.

Physical assets can therefore exist while usable system output remains bounded by capacity elsewhere within the wider structure.

Global capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

I log global capacity constraints.

### DGCP™ Topic #2702: Resource Extraction Capacity Constraints

Resource extraction capacity constraints occur where the ability to extract physical resources limits the quantity of material entering downstream systems.

Mining equipment, wells, production facilities, labor, energy, water, transport, and supporting infrastructure interact with geological or biological resource systems.

Resource availability and extraction capacity therefore represent distinct layers within production structures.

Resource extraction capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

I log resource extraction capacity constraints.

### DGCP™ Topic #2703: Resource Processing Capacity Constraints

Resource processing capacity constraints occur where raw materials must pass through processing facilities before entering downstream production.

Crushing, concentration, separation, purification, treatment, and other transformation stages operate within finite physical facilities.

Available resources can therefore exceed the capacity of systems capable of converting them into usable intermediate materials.

Resource processing capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

I log resource processing capacity constraints.

### DGCP™ Topic #2704: Refining Capacity Constraints

Refining capacity constraints occur where specialized facilities determine the volume of material that can be converted into refined industrial inputs.

Petroleum, metals, minerals, chemicals, and other materials can require refining between extraction and downstream use.

The productive capacity of resource systems therefore depends on both raw material supply and available refining infrastructure.

Electricity generation capacity constraints occur where available generation resources bound the electrical output that can be supplied to connected systems.

Generation capacity is distributed across power plants, renewable installations, storage-linked systems, and other electricity-producing infrastructure.

Industrial, commercial, residential, transport, and digital loads therefore interact with the capacity of physical electricity generation systems.

### DGCP™ Topic #2706: Electricity Transmission Capacity Constraints

Electricity transmission capacity constraints occur where high-voltage networks limit the amount of electricity that can be transferred between generation resources and demand locations.

Transmission lines, substations, transformers, interconnectors, and control systems form the physical pathways through which large-scale electricity flows.

Generation capacity and demand capacity can therefore remain geographically separated by transmission capacity.

Electricity transmission capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

I log electricity transmission capacity constraints.

### DGCP™ Topic #2707: Electricity Distribution Capacity Constraints

Electricity distribution capacity constraints occur where local networks limit the delivery of electricity from wider grids to individual users and facilities.

Substations, transformers, feeders, cables, switching equipment, and local network structures connect upstream electricity systems with downstream loads.

Available generation and transmission capacity therefore do not independently determine the electricity that can be delivered at a specific location.

Electricity distribution capacity constraints remain structurally observable.

Logistics handling capacity constraints occur where terminals, warehouses, distribution centers, cranes, loading systems, storage areas, and material-handling equipment bound cargo movement.

Goods can reach a transport node while still requiring transfer, sorting, storage, consolidation, or redistribution before continuing through the network.

Logistics capacity therefore includes both movement capacity and the capacity to process goods at intermediate nodes.

Storage separates production, transport, processing, and consumption across time.

Permitting, licensing, procurement, planning, inspection, coordination, data management, and other institutional functions require people, procedures, information, and operating structures.

Physical and financial resources therefore interact with institutional capacity before some forms of system activity can be executed.

### DGCP™ Topic #2719: Multi-Layer Capacity Constraints

Multi-layer capacity constraints occur where system output is bounded by several connected capacity layers rather than by a single resource or facility.

Resources can require extraction, processing, energy, water, manufacturing, transport, storage, labor, digital infrastructure, and institutional capacity before reaching downstream use.

Expansion at one layer therefore does not independently define total usable capacity across the complete system.

Global capacity constraint integration connects resource extraction, processing, refining, electricity, water, industry, manufacturing equipment, semiconductors, compute, connectivity, transport, logistics, storage, workforce, and institutional capacity.

Global systems operate through chains of finite physical and organizational capacities distributed across multiple sectors and geographic locations.
