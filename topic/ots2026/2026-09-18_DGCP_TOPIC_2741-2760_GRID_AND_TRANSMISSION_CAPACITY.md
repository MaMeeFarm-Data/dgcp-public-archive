### DGCP™ Topic #2741: Grid & Transmission Capacity

Grid and transmission capacity connects electricity generation with the physical networks capable of transferring electrical power across geographic areas.

Transmission lines, substations, transformers, interconnectors, control systems, and supporting infrastructure form pathways between generation resources and demand locations.

Installed generation therefore remains distinct from the capacity of electricity networks to transfer usable power between system nodes.

Grid and transmission capacity remains structurally observable.

### DGCP™ Topic #2742: High-Voltage Transmission Capacity

High-voltage transmission capacity connects major generation resources, substations, regional networks, and large demand centers through high-voltage electricity infrastructure.

Transmission circuits carry electrical power across distances while operating within physical and technical limits.

The amount of electricity available at one location therefore remains distinct from the amount that transmission infrastructure can transfer toward another location.

Transmission line capacity connects electricity flows with the physical characteristics of individual transmission circuits.

Conductors, towers, cables, voltage levels, thermal characteristics, surrounding infrastructure, and operating conditions interact with usable transfer capacity.

Transmission corridor capacity connects multiple electricity transmission assets within shared geographic pathways.

Transmission lines can occupy corridors shaped by geography, land access, infrastructure routes, substations, and connections with surrounding networks.

Electricity transfer between regions therefore depends on both individual circuits and the wider corridor structures through which those circuits pass.

### DGCP™ Topic #2745: Substation Capacity

Substation capacity connects transmission and distribution networks through facilities that transform, switch, protect, control, and route electrical power.

Transformers, busbars, switchgear, protection systems, control equipment, and incoming or outgoing circuits form interconnected substation structures.

Electricity transfer capacity therefore depends on intermediate nodes as well as transmission lines between them.

Transformer capacity connects electricity networks operating at different voltage levels.

Power transformers transfer electrical energy between transmission, subtransmission, distribution, industrial, and facility-level systems.

### DGCP™ Topic #2759: Multi-Layer Grid Capacity Constraints

Multi-layer grid capacity constraints occur where electricity transfer is bounded simultaneously by transmission lines, corridors, substations, transformers, interconnectors, connection points, equipment, and control infrastructure.

Capacity available at one grid layer does not independently determine the transfer capacity of the complete electricity pathway.

Usable electricity movement therefore emerges from connected capacity across multiple physical and operational network layers.
