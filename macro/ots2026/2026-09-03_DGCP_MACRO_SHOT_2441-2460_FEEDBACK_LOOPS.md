### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2441: Feedback Loops

Feedback loops occur when outputs, effects, measurements, or changed conditions return to influence subsequent system behavior.

Feedback may operate through physical processes, information systems, markets, institutions, human decisions, environmental conditions, or technical controls.

Repeated feedback can connect successive system states across time.

Feedback loops remain observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2442: Positive Feedback

Positive feedback occurs when a change contributes to conditions that reinforce further change in the same direction.

Increasing activity can generate additional demand, participation, investment, resource use, network growth, or system expansion.

Reinforcing relationships can continue while supporting conditions remain present.

Positive feedback remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2443: Negative Feedback

Negative feedback occurs when changing conditions generate responses that counter or reduce the original change.

Control systems, resource limits, institutional responses, price adjustments, capacity management, and behavioral changes can contribute to balancing effects.

These interactions can alter the rate or extent of system change.

Negative feedback remains observable.
