### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2861: Concentration Dynamics

Concentration dynamics describe how resources, capacity, activity, infrastructure, information, control, or flows become distributed around a limited number of locations, participants, nodes, or systems.

Concentration can emerge through geography, scale, connectivity, specialization, resource distribution, network structure, or institutional arrangements.

Different forms of concentration can coexist across interconnected systems.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2880: Concentration Dynamics Framework

Concentration dynamics connect resources, capacity, infrastructure, production, suppliers, demand, markets, networks, nodes, flows, geography, information, technology, institutions, and ownership.

Different forms of concentration can overlap when the same participants, locations, infrastructures, or nodes perform multiple system functions.

Concentration accumulation describes how clustering can extend across system layers while concentration reconfiguration describes changes in the distribution of concentrated activity.

Together these relationships reveal how system functions and capabilities become distributed around limited sets of participants, locations, nodes, networks, and structures.
