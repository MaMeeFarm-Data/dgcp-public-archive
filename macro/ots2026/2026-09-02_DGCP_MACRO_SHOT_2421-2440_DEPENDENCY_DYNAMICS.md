### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2421: Dependency Dynamics

Dependency dynamics describe how relationships of reliance operate and change within interconnected systems.

Systems depend on resources, infrastructure, information, services, institutions, capabilities, and other supporting functions.

Dependency structures may strengthen, weaken, shift, expand, contract, or become redistributed as operating conditions change.

Dependency dynamics remain observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2422: Direct Dependency

Direct dependency occurs when a system function relies immediately on a specific resource, service, component, or capability.

Electricity may directly support digital infrastructure, pumping systems, industrial equipment, transportation systems, and communications.

The relationship between dependent function and supporting input can often be identified through operational connections.

Direct dependency remains observable.

I log direct dependency.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2423: Indirect Dependency

Indirect dependency occurs when a system relies on supporting functions through intermediate components or networks.

A production facility may depend indirectly on telecommunications through logistics coordination, payment systems, suppliers, maintenance services, or workforce communication.

Indirect relationships can extend dependency structures beyond visible operational boundaries.

Indirect dependency remains observable.

I log indirect dependency.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2424: Upstream Dependency

Upstream dependency connects a system with resources, inputs, services, infrastructure, or processes required before its own function can operate.

Production may depend on energy, materials, water, equipment, data, transportation, finance, and labor.

Multiple upstream dependencies can converge on a single operational function.

Upstream dependency remains observable.

I log upstream dependency.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2425: Downstream Dependency

Downstream dependency connects outputs from one system with users, processes, networks, or functions that rely on those outputs.

Electricity generation supports distribution networks and connected users while production systems support downstream processing, transport, distribution, and consumption.

Downstream relationships extend the operational significance of system outputs.

Downstream dependency remains observable.

I log downstream dependency.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2426: Reciprocal Dependency

Reciprocal dependency occurs when two or more systems rely on one another.

Digital infrastructure may depend on electricity while electricity systems may depend on digital monitoring, communications, control systems, and data services.

Mutual reliance creates bidirectional dependency structures.

Reciprocal dependency remains observable.

I log reciprocal dependency.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2427: Multi-System Dependency

A single function may depend simultaneously on multiple systems.

Modern logistics can rely on transportation infrastructure, energy, communications, digital platforms, finance, labor, storage, navigation, and institutional processes.

These combined relationships create multi-system dependency structures.

Multi-system dependency remains observable.

I log multi-system dependency.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2428: Shared Dependency

Shared dependency occurs when multiple systems rely on the same resource, infrastructure, node, service, or capability.

Ports, data centers, industrial facilities, transportation networks, and settlements may share electricity, communications, water, transport corridors, or financial services.

Shared dependencies connect otherwise separate systems through common supporting structures.

Shared dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2429: Dependency Concentration

Dependency concentration occurs when reliance becomes focused on a limited number of resources, suppliers, nodes, routes, facilities, technologies, or locations.

Concentrated structures can emerge through geography, infrastructure design, network configuration, specialization, or limited alternatives.

The degree of concentration can be observed through dependency relationships.

Dependency concentration remains observable.

I log dependency concentration.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2430: Dependency Diversification

Dependency diversification occurs when a function is supported through multiple resources, suppliers, routes, technologies, locations, or operational alternatives.

Different supporting pathways may perform similar or complementary roles.

Diversification changes the distribution of reliance across the wider system.

Dependency diversification remains observable.

I log dependency diversification.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2431: Dependency Depth

Dependency depth describes the number of connected layers separating a system function from underlying supporting resources or capabilities.

A digital service may depend on computing infrastructure, which depends on electricity, cooling, telecommunications, equipment, maintenance, and material supply systems.

Dependency chains can therefore extend across multiple operational layers.

Dependency depth remains observable.

I log dependency depth.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2432: Dependency Chains

Dependency chains connect sequential relationships in which one function relies on another supporting function.

Resources may support production, production may support processing, processing may support distribution, and distribution may support final use.

Each connection extends the dependency structure across the system.

Dependency chains remain observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2433: Dependency Networks

Dependency networks form when multiple dependency chains connect through shared nodes, services, infrastructure, and functions.

Individual systems can become embedded within wider networks of reliance.

Network structures reveal how dependencies intersect across sectors, organizations, and geographic regions.

Dependency networks remain observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2434: Temporal Dependency

Dependencies can vary across time.

Seasonal conditions, operating cycles, maintenance periods, production schedules, demand patterns, and resource availability can alter the intensity or timing of reliance.

A dependency may therefore become more or less operationally significant under different temporal conditions.

Temporal dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2435: Spatial Dependency

Spatial dependency connects system functions with resources, infrastructure, facilities, routes, and services located in particular geographic areas.

Distance, accessibility, network connectivity, physical geography, and infrastructure distribution influence these relationships.

Spatial arrangements shape how dependency structures extend across territories.

Spatial dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2436: Capacity Dependency

System functions may depend not only on the presence of supporting infrastructure but also on available capacity.

Electricity supply, transport networks, ports, communications, storage, processing facilities, and water systems operate within finite capacities.

Changing utilization can alter the effective relationship between dependent functions and available support.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2437: Dependency Substitution

Dependency substitution occurs when one supporting resource, supplier, route, technology, process, or capability is replaced by another.

Substitution may alter cost structures, operating requirements, interfaces, geographic connections, or technical configurations.

The dependency remains while the supporting relationship changes.

Dependency substitution remains observable.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2438: Dependency Migration

Dependency migration occurs when reliance shifts from one system, location, supplier, technology, or resource toward another.

Infrastructure development, technological change, organizational restructuring, resource availability, and changing operating conditions can accompany such shifts.

Dependency structures can therefore move across systems and geography over time.

Dependency migration remains observable.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2439: Dependency Reconfiguration

Dependency reconfiguration occurs when existing relationships of reliance are reorganized.

New routes, infrastructure, suppliers, technologies, interfaces, or operational arrangements can change how supporting functions connect.

Reconfiguration modifies the structure of dependency without necessarily removing dependency itself.

Dependency reconfiguration remains observable.

I log dependency reconfiguration.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2440: Dependency Dynamics Framework

Dependency dynamics connect direct, indirect, upstream, downstream, reciprocal, shared, and multi-system relationships.

Dependencies form chains and networks across resources, infrastructure, capacity, technology, geography, institutions, and time.

Concentration, diversification, substitution, migration, and reconfiguration change how reliance is distributed across interconnected systems.

Together these relationships reveal dependency as a dynamic structure rather than a fixed connection.
