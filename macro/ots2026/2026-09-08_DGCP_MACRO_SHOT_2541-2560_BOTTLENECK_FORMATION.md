### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2541: Bottleneck Formation

Bottleneck formation occurs when activity becomes limited at a particular component, node, pathway, process, interface, or system layer.

Bottlenecks emerge where required flows encounter capacity, resource, timing, infrastructure, technical, institutional, or operational limits.

The location and intensity of a bottleneck can change as conditions elsewhere within the system change.

Bottleneck formation remains observable.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2542: Capacity Bottlenecks

Capacity bottlenecks occur when activity reaches the available capability of a component, facility, network, or process.

Production lines, ports, transport corridors, electricity networks, storage facilities, computing systems, and processing infrastructure operate within finite capacities.

When incoming or required activity exceeds available processing capability, the capacity limit becomes operationally visible.

Capacity bottlenecks remain observable.

I log capacity bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2543: Resource Bottlenecks

Resource bottlenecks occur when the availability or delivery of required inputs limits system activity.

Energy, water, materials, components, land, capital, information, and labor can become limiting inputs within different operational systems.

Resource availability can therefore determine how much existing infrastructure or productive capacity can be used.

Resource bottlenecks remain observable.

I log resource bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2544: Infrastructure Bottlenecks

Infrastructure bottlenecks occur where available infrastructure cannot support the volume, rate, connectivity, or configuration required by system activity.

Roads, railways, ports, pipelines, electricity networks, telecommunications, storage facilities, and digital infrastructure can contain localized limits.

Infrastructure bottlenecks can exist within systems that retain unused capacity elsewhere.

Infrastructure bottlenecks remain observable.

I log infrastructure bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2545: Node Bottlenecks

Node bottlenecks form where activity converges on a facility or connection point with limited processing, transfer, storage, or handling capability.

Ports, terminals, substations, warehouses, gateways, exchanges, data centers, and processing facilities can operate as high-traffic nodes.

Concentrated flows can make individual nodes structurally significant within wider networks.

Node bottlenecks remain observable.

I log node bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2546: Pathway Bottlenecks

Pathway bottlenecks occur when movement through a route, corridor, link, channel, pipeline, transmission line, or network connection becomes limiting.

Pathway width, throughput, infrastructure condition, operating rules, geography, and traffic levels influence available movement.

Alternative routes can contain different capacities and operational requirements.

Pathway bottlenecks remain observable.

I log pathway bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2547: Interface Bottlenecks

Interface bottlenecks occur where activity transfers between different components, systems, technologies, organizations, or operating environments.

Transfers may require compatible equipment, protocols, documentation, timing, handling capacity, permissions, or coordination.

A system can retain capacity on both sides of an interface while transfer capability remains limited.

Interface bottlenecks remain observable.

I log interface bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2548: Processing Bottlenecks

Processing bottlenecks occur when the rate of transformation, handling, verification, computation, settlement, approval, or other processing activity limits system throughput.

Incoming activity can accumulate when arrival rates exceed processing rates.

Processing capability therefore influences how quickly flows can continue through a system.

Processing bottlenecks remain observable.

I log processing bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2549: Temporal Bottlenecks

Temporal bottlenecks occur when timing, scheduling, sequencing, duration, or synchronization limits system activity.

Transport slots, production schedules, maintenance windows, institutional procedures, settlement periods, construction sequences, and operating hours can create time-based limits.

Capacity may exist physically while remaining unavailable at the required time.

Temporal bottlenecks remain observable.

I log temporal bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2550: Coordination Bottlenecks

Coordination bottlenecks occur when system activity is limited by the ability to align participants, information, resources, timing, decisions, or operational processes.

Complex activities can require multiple organizations or system components to act in compatible sequences.

Limited coordination can constrain activity even when physical resources and infrastructure remain available.

Coordination bottlenecks remain observable.

I log coordination bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2551: Information Bottlenecks

Information bottlenecks occur when the collection, transmission, processing, verification, or distribution of information limits subsequent activity.

Data availability, communication capacity, reporting processes, system compatibility, verification requirements, and information quality influence these limits.

Information bottlenecks can delay activity across physical and institutional systems.

Information bottlenecks remain observable.

I log information bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2552: Institutional Bottlenecks

Institutional bottlenecks occur when approvals, procedures, authority, regulation, documentation, standards, or organizational processes limit the rate or scale of activity.

Physical infrastructure and resources may be available while institutional processes remain sequential or capacity constrained.

Institutional throughput therefore forms part of wider system capacity.

Institutional bottlenecks remain observable.

I log institutional bottlenecks.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2553: Bottleneck Concentration

Bottleneck concentration occurs when a large portion of system activity depends on a limited number of constrained nodes, pathways, facilities, interfaces, or processes.

Network architecture, geography, specialization, infrastructure distribution, and operational design can create concentrated structures.

Concentration increases the amount of system activity passing through particular limiting points.

Bottleneck concentration remains observable.

I log bottleneck concentration.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2554: Bottleneck Accumulation

Bottleneck accumulation occurs when multiple limiting points exist across successive stages of a system.

Constraints in production, transportation, processing, storage, information, coordination, or distribution can coexist within the same flow structure.

Removing one limiting point can reveal another bottleneck elsewhere in the sequence.

Bottleneck accumulation remains observable.

I log bottleneck accumulation.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2555: Bottleneck Migration

Bottleneck migration occurs when the effective limiting point shifts from one component, process, node, pathway, or system layer to another.

Capacity expansion or constraint relaxation at one location can allow greater activity to reach a different limit downstream or upstream.

The system bottleneck can therefore move without disappearing.

Bottleneck migration remains observable.

I log bottleneck migration.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2556: Bottleneck Substitution

Bottleneck substitution occurs when one limiting condition is replaced by another as system conditions change.

Additional transport capacity may shift the effective limit toward ports, storage, production, energy, labor, or downstream distribution.

The identity of the dominant bottleneck depends on the relationship among connected capacities and constraints.

Bottleneck substitution remains observable.

I log bottleneck substitution.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2557: Bottleneck Amplification

Bottleneck amplification occurs when a localized limit contributes to larger accumulations, delays, utilization changes, or flow adjustments elsewhere within a connected system.

Queues can extend upstream while downstream components experience reduced incoming flows.

The operational effect can therefore extend beyond the physical location of the bottleneck.

Bottleneck amplification remains observable.

I log bottleneck amplification.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2558: Bottleneck Relief

Bottleneck relief occurs when the effective limit at a constrained component, node, pathway, interface, or process is reduced.

Additional capacity, alternative routes, expanded operating periods, improved coordination, additional resources, or changed system configuration can alter the limiting condition.

Relief at one point can redistribute activity toward other parts of the system.

Bottleneck relief remains observable.

I log bottleneck relief.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2559: Cross-System Bottlenecks

Cross-system bottlenecks occur when a limiting condition in one system restricts activity across other dependent systems.

Electricity constraints can limit industrial or computing activity while transportation constraints can limit material supply, production, and distribution.

Dependencies and coupling create pathways through which bottlenecks become relevant beyond their original system.

Cross-system bottlenecks remain observable.

I log cross-system bottlenecks.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2560: Bottleneck Formation Framework

Bottleneck formation connects capacity, resources, infrastructure, nodes, pathways, interfaces, processing, timing, coordination, information, and institutional limits across interconnected systems.

Concentration and accumulation describe how limiting points can become structurally significant while migration and substitution reveal how bottlenecks change location as system conditions change.

Amplification extends bottleneck effects across connected activities while relief can redistribute flows and expose different constraints elsewhere.

Together these structures reveal bottlenecks as dynamic limiting points that emerge, shift, interact, and reorganize activity across interconnected systems.
