### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2941: Stability & Instability

Stability and instability describe changing relationships between system continuity and structural variation.

Systems can maintain recognizable functions while components, flows, resources, capacity, networks, and operating conditions continue to change.

Instability can emerge when variations alter relationships faster or more extensively than existing structures absorb or redistribute them.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2960: Stability & Instability Framework

Stability and instability connect structures, operations, flows, capacity, resources, networks, institutions, dependencies, and system interactions.

Dynamic stability describes continuity through continuous adjustment while local and distributed instability describe different spatial and structural patterns of variation.

Transmission, amplification, and dampening describe how instability moves through connected systems while stability reconfiguration describes how continuity can emerge through changed arrangements.

Together these relationships reveal how interconnected systems maintain, lose, redistribute, and reorganize functional continuity under changing conditions.
