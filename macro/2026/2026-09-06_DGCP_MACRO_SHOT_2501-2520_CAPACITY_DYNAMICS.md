### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2501: Capacity Dynamics

Capacity dynamics describe how available, usable, occupied, reserve, and expandable capacity change within interconnected systems.

Infrastructure, production, logistics, energy, communications, storage, computing, institutions, and human systems operate within capacity structures.

Capacity conditions change as utilization, resources, configuration, maintenance, expansion, and system interactions change.

Capacity dynamics remain observable.

I log capacity dynamics.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2502: Installed Capacity

Installed capacity describes the capability represented by infrastructure, equipment, facilities, networks, or other operational assets that have been established within a system.

Installed assets define part of the structural capacity available to support system functions.

Installed capacity does not by itself establish how much capacity is usable or actively operating at a given time.

Installed capacity remains observable.

I log installed capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2503: Available Capacity

Available capacity describes the portion of system capacity that can be accessed under observed operating conditions.

Maintenance, outages, resource availability, staffing, network access, technical conditions, and operational requirements can affect availability.

Available capacity may therefore differ from total installed capacity.

Available capacity remains observable.

I log available capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2504: Usable Capacity

Usable capacity describes capacity that can perform a required function within existing technical, operational, spatial, or institutional conditions.

Infrastructure may exist while access, connectivity, compatibility, resources, or supporting services limit its effective use.

Usable capacity connects physical capability with operational accessibility.

Usable capacity remains observable.

I log usable capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2505: Utilized Capacity

Utilized capacity describes the portion of available capacity currently engaged in system activity.

Production facilities, transport networks, ports, storage systems, power infrastructure, telecommunications, and computing systems operate at varying levels of utilization.

Utilization changes as activity moves through the system.

Utilized capacity remains observable.

I log utilized capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2506: Spare Capacity

Spare capacity is the portion of available capacity not currently utilized.

Unused generation capability, processing capability, transport space, storage volume, network bandwidth, computing resources, or operational capability can form spare capacity.

The amount of spare capacity changes as utilization and availability change.

Spare capacity remains observable.

I log spare capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2507: Reserve Capacity

Reserve capacity is capacity maintained outside normal utilization for defined operational purposes.

Reserve structures may include standby equipment, backup infrastructure, additional inventory handling capability, redundant network resources, or alternative operating assets.

Reserve capacity differs according to system design and operating arrangements.

Reserve capacity remains observable.

I log reserve capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2508: Capacity Utilization

Capacity utilization describes the relationship between capacity in use and capacity available within a defined system.

Utilization can vary by asset, facility, network, sector, location, and time period.

Changes in utilization alter the amount of remaining capacity within the system.

Capacity utilization remains observable.

I log capacity utilization.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2509: Capacity Saturation

Capacity saturation occurs as system activity approaches the capacity available to support it.

Increasing utilization can reduce remaining operational space within infrastructure, facilities, networks, processing systems, or service structures.

Different parts of an interconnected system may reach saturation at different times.

Capacity saturation remains observable.

I log capacity saturation.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2510: Capacity Distribution

Capacity distribution describes how operational capability is arranged across assets, networks, facilities, organizations, technologies, and geographic locations.

Capacity may be concentrated within a limited number of nodes or distributed across multiple locations and systems.

Distribution influences where operational capability exists within the wider system.

Capacity distribution remains observable.

I log capacity distribution.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2511: Capacity Concentration

Capacity concentration occurs when a significant portion of operational capability is located within a limited number of facilities, nodes, networks, organizations, technologies, or geographic areas.

Industrial production, processing, logistics, computing, energy, and communications capacity can exhibit different levels of concentration.

Concentration can be observed through the distribution of installed and available capability.

Capacity concentration remains observable.

I log capacity concentration.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2512: Capacity Imbalance

Capacity imbalance occurs when capacity is unevenly distributed across connected parts of a system.

Production may exceed transport capacity, port capacity may exceed inland connectivity, or computing capability may exceed available power or network capacity.

Uneven capacity can alter how activity moves through interconnected systems.

Capacity imbalance remains observable.

I log capacity imbalance.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2513: Capacity Mismatch

Capacity mismatch occurs when connected system components provide different quantities, types, timings, or configurations of capacity.

Physical capacity may exist without compatible interfaces, supporting infrastructure, skilled labor, required inputs, or downstream processing capability.

Mismatch can therefore occur even when individual components retain unused capacity.

Capacity mismatch remains observable.

I log capacity mismatch.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2514: Capacity Flexibility

Capacity flexibility describes the ability of system capacity to shift among activities, locations, configurations, operating levels, or functions.

Flexible equipment, modular infrastructure, alternative routes, transferable resources, adaptable processes, and distributed networks can support changing capacity use.

The degree of flexibility varies across system structures.

Capacity flexibility remains observable.

I log capacity flexibility.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2515: Capacity Expansion

Capacity expansion occurs when additional operational capability is added to a system.

Expansion may involve new infrastructure, equipment, facilities, networks, workforce, storage, processing capability, computing resources, or supporting services.

Added capacity changes the structural limits within which system activity can operate.

Capacity expansion remains observable.

I log capacity expansion.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2516: Capacity Contraction

Capacity contraction occurs when operational capability is reduced or removed from a system.

Asset retirement, outages, maintenance, resource limitations, infrastructure loss, organizational closure, or reduced service availability can lower usable capacity.

Contraction changes the capacity available to connected activities.

Capacity contraction remains observable.

I log capacity contraction.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2517: Capacity Mobilization

Capacity mobilization occurs when existing but inactive, spare, reserve, or underutilized capability is brought into operation.

Additional shifts, equipment activation, network resources, storage facilities, transport assets, production lines, or organizational resources can increase active capacity.

Mobilization changes usable capacity without necessarily creating new installed capacity.

Capacity mobilization remains observable.

I log capacity mobilization.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2518: Capacity Response Time

Capacity response time describes the interval required for additional capability to become operational after changing system conditions create a requirement for it.

Response time can differ between activating existing capacity, reallocating resources, restoring unavailable capacity, and constructing new capability.

Technical, logistical, institutional, financial, and workforce processes influence observed response intervals.

Capacity response time remains observable.

I log capacity response time.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2519: Cross-System Capacity

Cross-system capacity describes the combined operational capability created by multiple connected systems.

Production capacity may depend on energy, transportation, communications, materials, labor, finance, water, logistics, and institutional capacity operating together.

The usable capability of one system can therefore be shaped by capacity conditions elsewhere.

Cross-system capacity remains observable.

I log cross-system capacity.

---

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2520: Capacity Dynamics Framework

Capacity dynamics connect installed, available, usable, utilized, spare, and reserve capacity across interconnected systems.

Utilization, saturation, distribution, concentration, imbalance, mismatch, flexibility, expansion, contraction, and mobilization describe observable changes in system capability.

Response time connects capacity with the temporal requirements of changing conditions while cross-system capacity reveals how capability depends on multiple supporting systems operating together.

Together these structures reveal capacity as a changing system condition rather than a fixed quantity.
