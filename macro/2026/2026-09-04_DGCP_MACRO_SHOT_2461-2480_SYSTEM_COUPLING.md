### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2461: System Coupling

System coupling occurs when components or systems become connected through relationships that allow conditions, actions, resources, information, or effects to pass between them.

Coupling may emerge through infrastructure, networks, interfaces, shared resources, operational processes, institutions, or human activity.

The strength and structure of these connections vary across interconnected systems.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2462: Direct Coupling

Direct coupling occurs when two components or systems are connected without an intermediate operational layer.

Physical connections, technical interfaces, resource transfers, data links, or coordinated processes can create direct relationships.

Changes at one side of the connection can enter the connected system through the shared interface.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2463: Indirect Coupling

Indirect coupling occurs when systems are connected through one or more intermediate components, networks, services, or processes.

A production system may connect with distant markets through transportation, logistics, communications, finance, and distribution infrastructure.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2464: Tight Coupling

Tight coupling occurs when connected components operate with limited separation in time, process, capacity, or operational sequence.

Limited separation can make interactions between connected components more immediate.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2465: Loose Coupling

Loose coupling occurs when connected components retain greater operational separation or flexibility.

Loose coupling allows connected functions to operate with reduced immediate synchronization.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2480: System Coupling Framework

System coupling connects components and systems through direct, indirect, physical, digital, operational, resource, infrastructure, network, spatial, temporal, capacity, institutional, and economic relationships.

Tight and loose coupling describe different degrees of operational separation while coupling density describes the distribution of connections across system structures.

Cross-domain relationships connect multiple layers while reconfiguration changes how those connections are organized over time.

Together these structures reveal how interconnected systems become linked through pathways that allow activity and changing conditions to move across system boundaries.
