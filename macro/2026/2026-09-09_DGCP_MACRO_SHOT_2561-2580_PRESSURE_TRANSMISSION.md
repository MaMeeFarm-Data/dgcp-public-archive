### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2561: Pressure Transmission

Pressure transmission describes how changing conditions in one part of a system become relevant to other connected components, processes, networks, or systems.

Pressure can originate from demand, capacity, resources, infrastructure, flows, timing, institutions, finance, information, environmental conditions, or human activity.

Dependencies, interfaces, networks, and shared resources provide pathways through which pressure can move across system boundaries.

Increasing demand, declining availability, rising utilization, resource limitations, congestion, operational changes, or environmental conditions can create pressure within different systems.

Pressure pathways are the relationships through which changing conditions become relevant elsewhere within a system.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2564: Direct Pressure Transmission

Direct pressure transmission occurs when changing conditions affect a connected component without passing through multiple intermediate system layers.

A capacity limit at one operational interface can directly alter the flow available to the next connected process.

Direct connections create identifiable pathways between pressure and subsequent system conditions.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2565: Indirect Pressure Transmission

Indirect pressure transmission occurs when pressure moves through intermediate components, processes, networks, institutions, or markets before becoming relevant elsewhere.

Indirect transmission can extend pressure across multiple system layers.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2580: Pressure Transmission Framework

Pressure transmission connects sources, pathways, dependencies, interfaces, networks, flows, resources, capacity, institutions, economic activity, and time across interconnected systems.

Pressure can move directly, indirectly, upstream, downstream, laterally, and across system boundaries while network structures determine how changing conditions are distributed.

Amplification and dampening alter transmission intensity while redistribution changes where pressure becomes concentrated within the system.

Together these structures reveal how changing conditions can move beyond their point of origin and become observable across connected components, networks, and system layers.
