### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2721: Substitution Dynamics

Substitution dynamics describe how one resource, input, technology, pathway, supplier, process, location, or capability can replace another within a system.

Substitution can emerge when availability, cost, capacity, access, performance, compatibility, timing, or operating conditions change.

The ability to substitute depends on structural relationships between the original function and available alternatives.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2722: Resource Substitution

Resource substitution occurs when one resource replaces another in supporting a system function.

Energy sources, materials, water sources, fuels, components, labor inputs, capital sources, and information resources can have different substitution relationships.

Technical requirements, availability, infrastructure, quality, and operating conditions influence whether substitution is possible.

Resource substitution remains observable.

I log resource substitution.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2723: Input Substitution

Input substitution occurs when an alternative input is used within a production, operational, service, or institutional process.

Inputs can differ in specification, quality, quantity, location, processing requirements, or compatibility.

Substitution therefore depends on whether the alternative can perform the required function within the existing system.

Input substitution remains observable.

I log input substitution.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2724: Supplier Substitution

Supplier substitution occurs when demand or procurement shifts from one source of supply to another.

Alternative suppliers can differ in capacity, location, transport requirements, standards, delivery schedules, contractual conditions, and production capability.

The existence of multiple suppliers does not necessarily imply equivalent substitutability.

Supplier substitution remains observable.

I log supplier substitution.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2725: Technology Substitution

Technology substitution occurs when one technological system, process, platform, or equipment configuration replaces another functionally related technology.

Substitution can require changes in infrastructure, skills, interfaces, standards, energy use, maintenance, software, or supporting resources.

Technology compatibility influences the scale and speed at which substitution can occur.

Technology substitution remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2726: Infrastructure Substitution

Infrastructure substitution occurs when activity shifts between alternative physical or digital infrastructure systems.

Transport modes, communication networks, energy systems, storage facilities, processing infrastructure, and distribution networks can provide alternative pathways for some functions.

Infrastructure capacity and connectivity determine how much activity can move between alternatives.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2727: Pathway Substitution

Pathway substitution occurs when flows move from one route, corridor, connection, channel, or network pathway to another.

Alternative pathways can differ in distance, capacity, timing, accessibility, cost, interfaces, and operating conditions.

Rerouting changes the distribution of activity across the wider network.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2728: Process Substitution

Process substitution occurs when a system function is performed through an alternative operational sequence or method.

Production, processing, verification, distribution, communication, settlement, administration, or service delivery can sometimes be performed through different processes.

Alternative processes can require different resources, infrastructure, skills, timing, and interfaces.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2729: Capacity Substitution

Capacity substitution occurs when available capability in one component, facility, network, location, or system replaces unavailable or constrained capability elsewhere.

Alternative production sites, transport routes, storage facilities, computing resources, service providers, or operational units can provide substitute capacity.

The usable amount depends on compatibility, connectivity, spare capacity, and access.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2730: Spatial Substitution

Spatial substitution occurs when activity shifts between geographic locations.

Production, sourcing, storage, processing, computing, logistics, services, or institutional functions can move between locations where alternatives exist.

Geography, infrastructure, resources, connectivity, regulation, and operating conditions influence spatial substitutability.

Spatial substitution remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2731: Temporal Substitution

Temporal substitution occurs when activity is shifted between different periods rather than performed at its original time.

Production schedules, energy use, transport activity, processing, maintenance, computing workloads, and consumption can sometimes move across time.

Scheduling flexibility and operational requirements determine the available range of temporal substitution.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2732: Functional Substitution

Functional substitution occurs when an alternative component or system performs the required function through a different structure or mechanism.

The substitute does not need to replicate the original component if it can provide the required system function.

Functional equivalence therefore differs from physical or technological similarity.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2733: Partial Substitution

Partial substitution occurs when an alternative replaces only part of the function, quantity, capacity, or demand previously supported by another component.

Alternative resources or systems can provide limited capability without replacing the original structure completely.

Partial substitution can distribute activity across multiple alternatives simultaneously.

Partial substitution remains observable.

I log partial substitution.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2734: Complete Substitution

Complete substitution occurs when an alternative can perform the required function without continued use of the original resource, component, pathway, technology, or process.

Complete substitution requires sufficient functional capability, capacity, compatibility, access, and supporting infrastructure.

Such conditions vary across different systems and operating environments.

Complete substitution remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2735: Substitution Capacity

Substitution capacity is the amount of activity that available alternatives can absorb or replace.

An alternative can exist while possessing insufficient capacity to accommodate the full volume of displaced activity.

Spare capacity, resource availability, infrastructure, connectivity, and operational capability influence substitution capacity.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2736: Substitution Friction

Substitution friction consists of structural conditions that limit or slow movement between alternatives.

Technical incompatibility, infrastructure requirements, switching processes, geography, standards, contracts, information, skills, timing, and limited capacity can create friction.

Substitution therefore operates within practical system constraints.

Substitution friction remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2737: Substitution Chains

Substitution chains occur when replacing one component creates additional substitution requirements elsewhere in the system.

A new resource can require different processing technology while a new route can require different transport connections, storage, or handling infrastructure.

Substitution can therefore extend through multiple connected system layers.

Substitution chains remain observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2738: Cross-System Substitution

Cross-system substitution occurs when an alternative from one system performs a function previously provided through another system.

Transport, energy, communication, production, digital, financial, or institutional functions can sometimes shift across technological or operational domains.

Cross-system substitution depends on functional compatibility and supporting system capacity.

Cross-system substitution remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2739: Substitution Reconfiguration

Substitution reconfiguration occurs when repeated or sustained substitution changes the distribution of resources, flows, capacity, infrastructure use, or dependencies within a system.

Activity can become concentrated around different suppliers, routes, technologies, locations, or operating processes.

Substitution therefore can alter system structure beyond the immediate replacement relationship.

Substitution reconfiguration remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2740: Substitution Dynamics Framework

Substitution dynamics connect resources, inputs, suppliers, technologies, infrastructure, pathways, processes, capacity, geography, time, and system functions.

Partial and complete substitution describe the extent of replacement while substitution capacity and friction describe the practical capability and constraints surrounding available alternatives.

Substitution chains connect replacement across multiple dependencies while cross-system substitution and reconfiguration reveal how alternatives can redistribute activity across wider system structures.

Together these relationships reveal how systems shift between available alternatives while preserving, modifying, or redistributing required functions across interconnected components and domains.
