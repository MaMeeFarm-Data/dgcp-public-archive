### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2701: Depletion Dynamics

Depletion dynamics describe how accumulated stocks decline when removals, consumption, losses, or outflows exceed additions over time.

Depletion can affect resources, inventories, reserves, stored energy, water, materials, capital, infrastructure capability, information capacity, or operational buffers.

The rate and duration of imbalance between inflows and outflows determine how accumulated quantities change.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2702: Stock Depletion

Stock depletion occurs when the quantity retained within a system decreases over an observed period.

Inventories, reservoirs, reserves, storage facilities, financial balances, material stocks, and other accumulated quantities can experience depletion.

Stock levels reflect the cumulative difference between additions and removals.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2703: Resource Depletion

Resource depletion occurs when use, extraction, consumption, loss, or removal reduces an available resource stock.

Water, energy resources, minerals, materials, biomass, land capability, and other resources operate within different replenishment and availability structures.

Resource conditions change as withdrawals interact with additions and regeneration.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2704: Inventory Depletion

Inventory depletion occurs when goods, materials, components, or supplies leave storage faster than they are replenished.

Consumption, production requirements, distribution, delivery schedules, procurement, and transport conditions influence inventory levels.

Inventory depletion changes the quantity available to support subsequent activity.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2705: Reserve Depletion

Reserve depletion occurs when quantities retained for additional, backup, emergency, or future use are progressively drawn down.

Energy reserves, financial reserves, material reserves, water storage, spare components, and other retained resources can provide temporary operating support.

Continued withdrawals reduce the remaining reserve available within the system.

Reserve depletion remains observable.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2706: Buffer Depletion

Buffer depletion occurs when stocks separating changing inflows and outflows are progressively consumed.

Inventories, storage, spare capacity, financial balances, intermediate materials, and operational reserves can function as buffers.

As buffers decline, connected processes become increasingly exposed to current flow conditions.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2707: Capacity Depletion

Capacity depletion occurs when usable capability declines through consumption, occupation, degradation, maintenance requirements, resource loss, or changing operating conditions.

Available storage, processing capability, infrastructure access, labor availability, equipment availability, or service capability can decrease over time.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2708: Spare Capacity Depletion

Spare capacity depletion occurs when previously unused capability becomes occupied by additional activity.

Increasing demand, rerouted flows, substitution, emergency operations, or expanding utilization can consume available operating margin.

As spare capacity declines, less unused capability remains within the system.

Spatial depletion occurs when declining stocks or capabilities become concentrated within particular geographic locations.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2712: Temporal Depletion

Temporal depletion occurs when stocks or capabilities decline progressively across time.

Small recurring differences between withdrawals and replenishment can create substantial cumulative reductions when sustained.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2713: Sequential Depletion

Sequential depletion occurs when declining availability moves through successive stocks, reserves, alternatives, or operating buffers.

Primary inventories can be consumed before reserve stocks while preferred resources can be used before substitute sources.

The sequence of available stocks influences how depletion becomes visible across system stages.

Shared resource depletion occurs when multiple users draw from the same finite or replenishing stock.

Water systems, energy supplies, storage facilities, transport capacity, financial pools, material inventories, and common infrastructure can support multiple activities.

Combined withdrawals influence the remaining quantity available to all connected users.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2715: Cross-System Depletion

Cross-system depletion occurs when declining stocks or capabilities in one system alter available resources or operating conditions within another.

Water depletion can affect agriculture, industry, energy, and settlements while fuel or energy depletion can alter transport, logistics, production, and digital operations.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2716: Depletion Transmission

Depletion transmission occurs when declining availability within one component changes the rate or availability of resources, capacity, or activity elsewhere.

Reduced upstream stocks can lower downstream inflows while declining buffers can increase dependence on current supply.

Depletion acceleration occurs when the rate of stock or capability decline increases over time.

Increasing withdrawals, declining replenishment, rising utilization, compounding losses, or simultaneous demand can increase the rate of reduction.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2718: Depletion Deceleration

Depletion deceleration occurs when the rate of stock or capability decline decreases over time.

Reduced withdrawals, increased replenishment, substitution, changing activity, conservation, operational adjustment, or reduced accessibility can slow depletion.

Depletion reconfiguration occurs when declining availability changes how resources, capacity, flows, or activities are distributed across a system.

Users can shift between stocks, locations, pathways, technologies, suppliers, or operating modes as availability changes.

These shifts alter where remaining resources and system activity become concentrated.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2720: Depletion Dynamics Framework

Depletion dynamics connect stocks, resources, inventories, reserves, buffers, capacity, withdrawals, replenishment, space, and time across interconnected systems.

Rates, uneven distribution, sequential drawdown, and shared resource use describe how declining quantities develop within different structural conditions.

Transmission connects depletion across dependencies while acceleration, deceleration, and reconfiguration describe changes in the rate and distribution of declining availability.

Together these relationships reveal how accumulated system capability can be progressively reduced and redistributed as outflows, consumption, losses, and replenishment interact over time.
