### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2521: Constraint Dynamics

Constraint dynamics describe how limits emerge, interact, shift, accumulate, and change within interconnected systems.

Constraints can arise from resources, capacity, infrastructure, geography, technology, institutions, time, information, finance, labor, or environmental conditions.

A constraint affecting one system can interact with conditions elsewhere through dependencies and shared operational structures.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2522: Resource Constraints

Resource constraints occur when access to materials, energy, water, land, capital, information, labor, or other required inputs limits system activity.

Resource availability can vary across locations, operating periods, networks, and users.

Changing resource conditions can alter the activities supported by connected systems.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2523: Capacity Constraints

Capacity constraints occur when available system capability limits the quantity or rate of activity that can be supported.

Production facilities, transport networks, ports, storage systems, energy infrastructure, communications, computing, and institutional processes operate within finite capacities.

Increasing utilization can bring activity closer to those limits.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2524: Infrastructure Constraints

Infrastructure constraints occur when physical or digital infrastructure limits connectivity, throughput, access, processing, storage, movement, or service availability.

Shared constraints occur when multiple systems encounter limits associated with the same resource, infrastructure, location, service, or capability.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2534: Constraint Interaction

Constraint interaction occurs when two or more limits affect one another within interconnected systems.

Limited transport capacity can interact with storage constraints while energy limitations can interact with production, computing, water, or communications capacity.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2535: Constraint Accumulation

Constraint accumulation occurs when multiple limits build across successive components, processes, locations, or operating periods.

Small limitations in resources, capacity, timing, infrastructure, information, or coordination can coexist across a system.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2536: Constraint Migration

Constraint migration occurs when a limiting condition shifts from one component, location, process, or system layer to another.

Expanding one part of a network may remove an existing limit while revealing a different constraint elsewhere.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2537: Constraint Substitution

Constraint substitution occurs when resolving or reducing one limitation leaves another constraint as the effective operating limit.

Additional production capacity may increase output until transportation, storage, energy, labor, or downstream processing becomes limiting.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2538: Constraint Relaxation

Constraint relaxation occurs when a limiting condition is reduced through additional resources, capacity, infrastructure, technology, access, coordination, or operational change.

Relaxation can increase the range or volume of activity supported by a system.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2539: Cross-System Constraints

Cross-system constraints occur when limitations within one system affect the operating conditions of other connected systems.

Electricity capacity can constrain computing or industrial activity while transportation capacity can constrain production and distribution.

Dependencies provide pathways through which constraints become relevant beyond their system of origin.

### DGCP™ Macro Shot #2540: Constraint Dynamics Framework

Constraint dynamics connect resource, capacity, infrastructure, geographic, temporal, technical, information, institutional, financial, labor, and environmental limits across interconnected systems.

Shared constraints and constraint interactions reveal how multiple limits can operate simultaneously while accumulation, migration, substitution, and relaxation describe how limiting conditions change across system states.

Cross-system relationships allow constraints to extend beyond their point of origin through dependencies, interfaces, and shared operational structures.

Together these structures reveal constraints as dynamic conditions that can shift across components, networks, locations, and time.
