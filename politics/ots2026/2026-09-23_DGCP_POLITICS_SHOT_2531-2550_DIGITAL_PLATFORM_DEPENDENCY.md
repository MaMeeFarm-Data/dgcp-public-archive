### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2531: Digital Platform Dependency

Digital platform dependency exists when political, economic, institutional, or social systems rely on digital platforms to connect users, organizations, services, transactions, information, or applications.

Platforms can coordinate activities that previously occurred through separate technical or organizational systems.

Visible platform services depend on underlying infrastructure including networks, cloud systems, data centers, software, identity, payments, and electricity.

Digital platform dependency therefore requires observation of both the platform layer and the infrastructure beneath it.

Digital platform dependency remains observable.

I log digital platform dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2532: Platform Access Dependency

Platform access dependency emerges when individuals, businesses, institutions, or developers rely on access to particular digital platforms for participation in digital activities.

Access can involve accounts, credentials, devices, applications, networks, contractual relationships, or technical interfaces.

The existence of internet connectivity therefore does not automatically establish access to every platform-based function.

Platform access dependency connects digital participation with platform infrastructure.

Platform access dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2549: Cross-System Digital Platform Dependency

Dependency therefore operates both beneath platform systems and outward into activities coordinated through them.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2550: Digital Platform Dependency Architecture

Digital platform dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, institutional, and social systems rely on platform access, identity, data, cloud infrastructure, networks, software, APIs, application distribution, commerce, payments, logistics, communications, information distribution, developer ecosystems, business relationships, and jurisdictional structures.

Digital platforms combine technical, infrastructural, commercial, financial, informational, institutional, geographic, and human layers.

Dependency can be infrastructural, platform-based, provider-based, technical, commercial, jurisdictional, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider digital systems.

The existence of digital platform dependency does not by itself establish market dominance, political control, censorship, coercion, vulnerability, exclusion, disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Digital platform dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.

The overall digital platform dependency architecture remains observable.

I log the digital platform dependency architecture.
