### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2151: Energy Dependency

Energy dependency exists when political, economic, industrial, social, or security systems rely on external energy resources, technologies, infrastructure, equipment, or networks.

Energy systems include fuels, electricity, generation capacity, transmission networks, storage, processing, transportation, and distribution.

A state can possess domestic energy resources while remaining dependent on external technologies or infrastructure.

Energy dependency therefore requires observation across the complete energy system rather than fuel imports alone.

Energy dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2152: Oil Dependency

Oil dependency emerges when transportation, industry, petrochemicals, power generation, or other economic functions rely substantially on crude oil or refined petroleum products.

Dependency can exist through crude imports, refined-product imports, refining capacity, transport infrastructure, storage, or access to international markets.

Domestic production does not necessarily eliminate dependency on refining or distribution systems.

Oil dependency therefore extends across both resource supply and downstream infrastructure.

Oil dependency remains observable.

Gas can move through pipelines or liquefied natural gas infrastructure.

Import terminals, pipelines, storage, processing facilities, shipping capacity, and contracts can form different dependency layers.

Electricity import dependency emerges when domestic electricity systems rely on power supplied through cross-border interconnections.

Electricity imports can support base demand, peak demand, balancing, regional trade, or specific geographic areas.

Cross-border electricity flows depend on transmission infrastructure, technical coordination, and institutional arrangements.

Electricity import dependency therefore connects national grids with regional power systems.

Electricity generation dependency exists when power systems rely on specific fuels, technologies, equipment, or external supply chains to maintain generation capacity.

Coal plants, gas plants, nuclear reactors, hydropower, solar systems, wind systems, and other technologies contain different dependency structures.

Domestic generation capacity can therefore remain dependent on imported fuels or technologies.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2158: Grid Infrastructure Dependency

Grid infrastructure dependency emerges when energy systems rely on transmission lines, substations, transformers, control systems, distribution networks, and balancing infrastructure.

Electricity generation cannot support users without functioning grid infrastructure.

Grid equipment and control technologies can also depend on specialized external suppliers.

Grid dependency therefore links power availability with infrastructure and technical capability.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2159: Transformer and Grid Equipment Dependency

Transformer and grid equipment dependency exists when electricity systems rely on specialized transformers, switchgear, cables, protection systems, power electronics, or other grid equipment.

Manufacturing capacity for some grid components can be concentrated among limited suppliers.

Replacement cycles, technical specifications, and compatibility can further shape dependency.

Grid equipment dependency therefore forms an industrial layer within electricity infrastructure.

Transformer and grid equipment dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2160: Renewable Technology Dependency

Renewable technology dependency emerges when energy expansion relies on imported solar panels, wind turbines, inverters, batteries, power electronics, control systems, or specialized components.

Renewable resources such as sunlight and wind may be domestic while technology supply chains remain international.

Manufacturing, materials, intellectual property, equipment, and installation capability can be distributed across several jurisdictions.

Renewable energy dependency therefore differs from direct fuel dependency.

Energy chokepoint dependency emerges when substantial energy flows pass through limited geographic routes, terminals, pipelines, canals, straits, or infrastructure nodes.

Concentrated energy movement can occur even when producers and consumers are geographically diversified.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2168: Cross-Border Energy Dependency

Cross-border energy dependency emerges when energy systems connect states through electricity interconnectors, pipelines, fuel trade, shared infrastructure, transit routes, or regional markets.

Producing, transit, importing, and interconnected states occupy different positions within these networks.

A single energy system can therefore contain several jurisdictions with distinct roles.

Cross-border energy dependency reflects the international structure of energy movement and access.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2169: Energy Dependency Concentration

Energy dependency concentration occurs when an important fuel, technology, component, supplier, route, infrastructure node, or processing capability is concentrated within a limited number of sources.

Diversification at one layer does not necessarily eliminate concentration elsewhere.

A state may import fuels from several suppliers while depending on one technology, route, refinery system, or equipment category.

Energy dependency concentration therefore requires mapping across multiple layers simultaneously.

Energy dependency concentration remains observable.

I log energy dependency concentration.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2170: Energy Dependency Architecture

Energy dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political and economic systems depend on fuels, electricity, generation capacity, grids, technologies, equipment, minerals, transport, storage, and international energy networks.

Oil, gas, coal, nuclear fuel, electricity, renewable technologies, batteries, grid systems, minerals, pipelines, ports, shipping routes, and cross-border connections form overlapping layers.

Energy dependency can be domestic, external, direct, embedded, diversified, concentrated, technological, infrastructural, or resource-based.

The existence of energy dependency does not by itself establish vulnerability, coercion, political intent, disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Energy dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.

The overall energy dependency architecture remains observable.
