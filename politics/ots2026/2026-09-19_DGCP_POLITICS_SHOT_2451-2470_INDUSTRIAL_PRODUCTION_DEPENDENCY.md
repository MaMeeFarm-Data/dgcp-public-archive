### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2451: Industrial Production Dependency

Industrial production dependency exists when economic, technological, infrastructure, or institutional systems rely on interconnected capabilities required to transform materials and components into usable products.

Production systems combine factories, machinery, materials, components, energy, water, software, logistics, finance, and human capability.

The presence of final assembly therefore does not necessarily establish control over the complete production system.

Industrial production dependency requires observation across multiple upstream and supporting layers.

Industrial production dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2470: Industrial Production Dependency Architecture

Industrial production dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, technological, and infrastructure systems rely on manufacturing capacity, machinery, materials, components, semiconductors, energy, water, chemicals, automation, software, maintenance, spare parts, workforce, logistics, finance, and supplier networks.

Industrial production combines physical, material, technological, energy, water, digital, financial, logistical, institutional, and human-capability layers.

Dependency can be material, technological, infrastructural, supplier-based, geographic, financial, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider production systems.

The existence of industrial production dependency does not by itself establish vulnerability, industrial weakness, political control, coercion, production disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Industrial production dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.
