### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2231: Semiconductor Dependency

Semiconductor dependency exists when political, economic, industrial, digital, security, or technological systems rely on access to semiconductor design, manufacturing, equipment, materials, packaging, testing, or related capabilities.

Semiconductor production operates through multiple specialized layers distributed across firms and jurisdictions.

Possession of capability at one layer does not establish control over the complete production system.

Semiconductor dependency therefore requires mapping across the full semiconductor ecosystem.

Semiconductor dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2250: Semiconductor Dependency Architecture

Semiconductor dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, industrial, technological, digital, and security systems rely on chip design, software, intellectual property, fabrication, lithography, equipment, materials, packaging, testing, energy, water, logistics, and skilled labor.

Semiconductor production consists of specialized layers distributed across firms, facilities, industries, and jurisdictions.

Dependency can be technological, industrial, infrastructural, material, geographic, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within downstream systems.

The existence of semiconductor dependency does not by itself establish vulnerability, coercion, political intent, supply disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Semiconductor dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.
