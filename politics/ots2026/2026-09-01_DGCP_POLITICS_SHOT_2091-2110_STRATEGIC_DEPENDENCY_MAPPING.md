### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2091: Strategic Dependency Mapping

Strategic dependency mapping identifies systems, resources, capabilities, infrastructure, technologies, institutions, and external relationships on which political systems depend.

Dependencies can exist within domestic systems, across borders, or through networks extending across multiple jurisdictions.

The structure of a dependency can differ according to concentration, access, substitutability, ownership, control, and network position.

Strategic dependency mapping therefore separates the existence of dependency from assumptions about political intent or outcome.

Strategic dependency structures remain observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2092: Resource Dependency

Resource dependency emerges when states rely on access to resources located, produced, processed, or supplied through external systems.

Energy resources, minerals, agricultural commodities, water-related inputs, and industrial materials can form different dependency structures.

Resource availability does not alone determine dependency because processing capacity, transportation, storage, and alternative supply also shape access.

Resource dependency therefore extends across both physical resources and the systems required to make them usable.

Resource dependency remains observable.

I log resource dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2093: Infrastructure Dependency

Infrastructure dependency exists when political, economic, or social functions rely on physical systems required for movement, connectivity, energy, communication, or essential services.

Ports, pipelines, electricity grids, telecommunications networks, transport corridors, cables, and logistics facilities can connect multiple jurisdictions.

Infrastructure ownership, operation, location, financing, maintenance, and access can involve different actors.

Infrastructure dependency therefore connects physical systems with institutional and political relationships.

Infrastructure dependency remains observable.

I log infrastructure dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2094: Technology Dependency

Technology dependency emerges when important capabilities rely on technologies, equipment, software, intellectual property, technical knowledge, or production systems supplied externally.

Advanced technology ecosystems often contain specialized capabilities distributed across several companies and jurisdictions.

Dependency can exist at individual layers even when final products are assembled or operated domestically.

Technology dependency therefore requires observation across the complete technology stack rather than only final-product access.

Technology dependency remains observable.

I log technology dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2095: Industrial Dependency

Industrial dependency exists when production systems rely on external machinery, components, materials, intermediate goods, manufacturing capacity, or specialized industrial services.

Modern industrial systems frequently connect several stages of production across different jurisdictions.

Domestic production at one stage can coexist with external dependency at another stage.

Industrial dependency therefore reflects the structure of production networks rather than a simple distinction between domestic and foreign manufacturing.

Industrial dependency remains observable.

I log industrial dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2096: Energy Dependency

Energy dependency emerges when states rely on external sources, infrastructure, technologies, fuels, equipment, or networks to maintain energy supply.

Oil, natural gas, coal, nuclear fuel, electricity, renewable technologies, storage systems, and grid equipment create different dependency structures.

Energy dependency can exist across production, conversion, transportation, generation, storage, and distribution.

Energy systems therefore contain multiple layers of dependency beyond direct fuel imports.

Energy dependency remains observable.

I log energy dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2097: Food System Dependency

Food system dependency exists when food availability relies substantially on imported food, agricultural inputs, fertilizers, seeds, machinery, energy, logistics, processing, or external markets.

Domestic agricultural production can itself depend on internationally supplied inputs and infrastructure.

Food systems also connect production regions with processing, storage, transportation, and consumption networks.

Food dependency therefore extends beyond direct measurement of food imports.

Food system dependency remains observable.

I log food system dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2098: Water System Dependency

Water system dependency emerges when political and economic systems rely on water sources, upstream flows, shared basins, infrastructure, energy, treatment systems, or external technologies.

Rivers, aquifers, reservoirs, desalination systems, irrigation networks, and water-intensive supply chains create different forms of dependency.

Some water dependencies cross political borders while others remain embedded within domestic infrastructure and resource systems.

Water dependency therefore connects geography, infrastructure, production, and political jurisdiction.

Water system dependency remains observable.

I log water system dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2099: Financial Dependency

Financial dependency exists when states, institutions, firms, or essential systems rely on external capital, credit, currencies, payment infrastructure, investment, or financial institutions.

International financial networks connect borrowers, lenders, investors, markets, clearing systems, and monetary structures.

Different forms of financial dependency can operate simultaneously within the same economy.

Financial dependency therefore reflects access to financial systems as well as the structure of financial relationships.

Financial dependency remains observable.

I log financial dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2100: Trade Dependency

Trade dependency emerges when access to important goods, services, inputs, technologies, or markets depends substantially on international exchange.

Import dependency and export-market dependency represent different positions within trade networks.

Trade concentration can also occur around particular suppliers, buyers, routes, commodities, or jurisdictions.

Trade dependency therefore requires observation of both supply-side and market-side relationships.

Trade dependency remains observable.

I log trade dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2101: Logistics Dependency

Logistics dependency exists when economic and political systems rely on transportation networks, ports, shipping services, freight infrastructure, warehousing, border systems, or transit corridors.

Goods can depend on several logistics nodes between production and final consumption.

Alternative routes may differ in capacity, cost, distance, infrastructure, and jurisdictional access.

Logistics dependency therefore reflects the network structure connecting supply with physical movement.

Logistics dependency remains observable.

I log logistics dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2102: Digital Infrastructure Dependency

Digital infrastructure dependency emerges when government, economic, communication, or social functions rely on telecommunications networks, data centers, cloud infrastructure, cables, satellites, software, or digital services.

Digital systems can contain several physical and virtual layers operated by different organizations.

Domestic digital activity can therefore depend on infrastructure or services located outside national territory.

Digital infrastructure dependency connects technological systems with geography, ownership, access, and jurisdiction.

Digital infrastructure dependency remains observable.

I log digital infrastructure dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2103: Compute Dependency

Compute dependency exists when advanced digital functions rely on access to computing hardware, accelerators, data centers, cloud capacity, electricity, cooling, networks, software, or technical expertise.

Compute infrastructure connects semiconductor production with physical infrastructure and digital services.

Different jurisdictions can possess capability at some layers while depending externally on others.

Compute dependency therefore forms a multilayer structure rather than a single hardware relationship.

Compute dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2104: Knowledge and Skills Dependency

Knowledge and skills dependency emerges when important systems rely on specialized expertise, research capacity, education, technical labor, or professional capabilities that are limited domestically.

Highly specialized industries can depend on relatively small pools of trained personnel distributed internationally.

Migration, education, research institutions, firms, and professional networks can all influence access to expertise.

Knowledge dependency therefore connects human capability with institutional and economic systems.

Knowledge and skills dependency remains observable.

I log knowledge and skills dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2105: Institutional Dependency

Institutional dependency exists when political or economic functions rely on external rules, organizations, agreements, standards, certification systems, or governance arrangements.

States participate in multiple bilateral, regional, and multilateral institutional structures.

Access to markets, finance, security arrangements, technologies, or infrastructure can interact with these institutional relationships.

Institutional dependency therefore operates through formal structures connecting jurisdictions and organizations.

Institutional dependency remains observable.

I log institutional dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2106: Security Dependency

Security dependency emerges when states rely on external military capabilities, defense equipment, intelligence relationships, alliances, logistics, technology, or security guarantees.

Security systems can combine domestic capabilities with bilateral and multilateral arrangements.

Dependency can exist at specific layers such as equipment supply, maintenance, intelligence, communications, or strategic access.

Security dependency therefore requires observation of the architecture supporting security capability.

Security dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2107: Geographic Dependency

Geographic dependency exists when access to resources, markets, infrastructure, transportation routes, neighboring territories, or strategic spaces is shaped by physical location.

Landlocked states, island states, transit states, coastal states, and states located near strategic corridors occupy different geographic positions.

Geography can influence the number and type of available connections without independently determining political outcomes.

Geographic dependency therefore reflects the relationship between physical position and system access.

Geographic dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2108: Concentrated Dependency

Concentrated dependency occurs when an important resource, capability, supplier, infrastructure node, technology, route, or service is available through a limited number of sources.

Concentration can exist at global, regional, national, sectoral, or company levels.

A system can contain diversified final suppliers while retaining concentration deeper within production or infrastructure layers.

Concentrated dependency therefore requires mapping beyond visible first-tier relationships.

Concentrated dependency remains observable.

I log concentrated dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2109: Cross-System Dependency

Cross-system dependency occurs when one political or economic capability depends on several other systems operating together.

Technology can depend on energy, minerals, manufacturing, logistics, finance, skilled labor, infrastructure, and regulatory access simultaneously.

A dependency originating in one system can therefore connect structurally with dependencies located elsewhere.

Cross-system mapping reveals these connections without establishing that disruption, coercion, or political effect will occur.

Cross-system dependency remains observable.

I log cross-system dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2110: Strategic Dependency Architecture

Strategic dependency architecture describes the combined structure through which states depend on resources, infrastructure, technology, industry, energy, food, water, finance, trade, logistics, digital systems, compute, knowledge, institutions, security, and geography.

These dependencies can be domestic, bilateral, regional, global, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider networks.

States can simultaneously control important capabilities while remaining dependent on external systems at other layers.

Dependency alone does not establish vulnerability, coercion, political intent, or a specific political outcome.

Strategic dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable relationships from interpretation.

The overall strategic dependency architecture remains observable.
