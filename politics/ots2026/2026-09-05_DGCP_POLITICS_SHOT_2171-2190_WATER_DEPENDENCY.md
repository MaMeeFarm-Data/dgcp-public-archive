### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2171: Water Dependency

Water dependency exists when political, economic, social, agricultural, industrial, or energy systems rely on reliable access to usable water.

Water dependency can emerge through natural availability, shared basins, infrastructure, treatment capacity, energy supply, technology, or external relationships.

Physical water presence does not alone establish usable or secure access.

Water dependency therefore requires observation across resource, infrastructure, geography, and governance layers.

Water dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2172: Surface Water Dependency

Surface water dependency emerges when populations, agriculture, industry, or energy systems rely substantially on rivers, lakes, reservoirs, or other surface-water sources.

Surface-water systems can vary across seasons and geographic regions.

Many surface-water sources also cross administrative or national boundaries.

Surface water dependency therefore connects hydrology with infrastructure and political geography.

Surface water dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2173: Groundwater Dependency

Groundwater dependency exists when households, agriculture, industry, or public systems rely on aquifers and underground water sources.

Groundwater access depends on geology, wells, pumping systems, electricity, monitoring, and recharge conditions.

Aquifers can extend across administrative and national boundaries.

Groundwater dependency therefore combines natural systems with infrastructure and governance.

Groundwater dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2174: River Basin Dependency

River basin dependency emerges when multiple communities, sectors, regions, or states rely on the same river system.

Water use upstream and downstream occurs within a connected hydrological structure.

Dams, irrigation systems, cities, industries, ecosystems, and energy infrastructure can all depend on the same basin.

River basin dependency therefore creates interconnected geographic and political relationships.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2175: Transboundary Water Dependency

Transboundary water dependency exists when water resources cross political borders or are shared among multiple jurisdictions.

Rivers, lakes, and aquifers can create direct hydrological connections among states.

Management can involve treaties, commissions, technical coordination, or other institutional arrangements.

Transboundary water dependency therefore links natural geography with interstate relationships.

Transboundary water dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2176: Upstream Dependency

Upstream dependency relationships emerge when downstream access to water is structurally connected to hydrological conditions, infrastructure, or water management located upstream.

Upstream areas can contain headwaters, reservoirs, dams, irrigation systems, or diversion infrastructure.

The existence of upstream position does not by itself establish control over all downstream outcomes.

Upstream dependency therefore requires separation of geographic position from assumptions about political leverage.

Upstream dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2177: Reservoir Dependency

Reservoir dependency exists when water supply, irrigation, electricity generation, flood management, or urban systems rely on stored surface water.

Reservoirs connect natural water flows with engineered storage capacity.

Their operation depends on infrastructure, management, inflows, maintenance, and distribution systems.

Reservoir dependency therefore forms both a resource and infrastructure relationship.

Reservoir dependency remains observable.

I log reservoir dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2178: Dam Infrastructure Dependency

Dam infrastructure dependency emerges when water storage, hydropower, irrigation, flood management, or water regulation relies on dam systems.

Dams can serve several functions simultaneously.

Their operation connects water resources with electricity, agriculture, infrastructure, and downstream systems.

Dam dependency therefore represents a cross-system relationship rather than a single water function.

Dam infrastructure dependency remains observable.

I log dam infrastructure dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2179: Irrigation Dependency

Irrigation dependency exists when agricultural production relies on controlled water delivery beyond direct rainfall.

Irrigation systems include canals, pumps, reservoirs, wells, pipelines, and distribution networks.

Agricultural output can therefore depend on both water availability and supporting infrastructure.

Irrigation dependency connects food production with water, energy, and infrastructure systems.

Irrigation dependency remains observable.

I log irrigation dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2180: Urban Water Dependency

Urban water dependency emerges when concentrated populations and economic activity rely on continuous water capture, treatment, storage, pumping, and distribution.

Cities can depend on water sources located far beyond their administrative boundaries.

Urban water systems also rely on electricity, treatment chemicals, infrastructure, and maintenance capacity.

Urban water dependency therefore extends beyond local rainfall or nearby surface water.

Urban water dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2181: Industrial Water Dependency

Industrial water dependency exists when manufacturing, mining, processing, cooling, cleaning, or other production functions rely on significant water availability.

Different industries have different water-quality and water-volume requirements.

Industrial facilities can depend on municipal systems, private infrastructure, groundwater, rivers, reservoirs, or desalination.

Industrial water dependency therefore connects productive capacity with water access and infrastructure.

Industrial water dependency remains observable.

I log industrial water dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2182: Energy System Water Dependency

Energy system water dependency emerges when electricity generation, fuel processing, cooling, hydropower, or other energy functions rely on water.

Thermal power generation, nuclear generation, hydropower, fuel extraction, and some renewable supply chains contain different water requirements.

Energy and water systems therefore interact directly at multiple layers.

Energy system water dependency forms part of the wider water-energy nexus.

Energy system water dependency remains observable.

I log energy system water dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2183: Food System Water Dependency

Food system water dependency exists when agricultural production, livestock, processing, and food supply rely on adequate water availability.

Rainfall, irrigation, groundwater, reservoirs, rivers, and water infrastructure can all contribute to agricultural water access.

Food imports can also embed water dependency through production occurring in other regions.

Food system water dependency therefore can be both domestic and externally embedded.

Food system water dependency remains observable.

I log food system water dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2184: Desalination Dependency

Desalination dependency emerges when water supply relies on technologies that convert seawater or brackish water into usable freshwater.

Desalination systems depend on energy, treatment equipment, membranes, chemicals, intake infrastructure, maintenance, and distribution networks.

Water access can therefore depend on technology and energy even where seawater availability is abundant.

Desalination dependency links water systems with industrial and energy infrastructure.

Desalination dependency remains observable.

I log desalination dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2185: Water Treatment Dependency

Water treatment dependency exists when usable water relies on filtration, purification, disinfection, chemical treatment, or wastewater-processing systems.

Raw water availability does not necessarily provide water suitable for households, industry, agriculture, or public services.

Treatment systems depend on infrastructure, electricity, chemicals, equipment, and technical expertise.

Water treatment dependency therefore forms an important layer between water resources and final use.

Water treatment dependency remains observable.

I log water treatment dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2186: Pumping and Distribution Dependency

Pumping and distribution dependency emerges when water access relies on pumps, pipelines, canals, pressure systems, storage tanks, and distribution infrastructure.

Water sources can remain physically available while being inaccessible without functioning transport and pumping systems.

Many of these systems also depend on electricity and maintenance capability.

Water distribution dependency therefore connects physical water availability with infrastructure operation.

Pumping and distribution dependency remains observable.

I log pumping and distribution dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2187: Water Technology Dependency

Water technology dependency exists when water supply, treatment, desalination, monitoring, irrigation, leakage management, or reuse relies on specialized technologies.

Equipment, membranes, sensors, pumps, control systems, software, chemicals, and engineering expertise can come from different suppliers and jurisdictions.

Domestic water resources can therefore coexist with external technology dependency.

Water technology dependency remains observable.

I log water technology dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2188: Water Infrastructure Concentration

Water infrastructure concentration occurs when major populations, sectors, or regions depend heavily on a limited number of reservoirs, treatment plants, canals, pumping stations, pipelines, or water-transfer systems.

Large distributed water systems can still contain concentrated critical nodes.

Concentration can exist at supply, treatment, storage, transfer, or distribution layers.

Water infrastructure concentration therefore requires network-level observation.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2189: Cross-System Water Dependency

Cross-system water dependency occurs when water systems interact structurally with energy, agriculture, industry, urban infrastructure, technology, logistics, and public services.

Water pumping can depend on electricity.

Electricity generation can depend on water.

Food production can depend on both water and energy.

Cross-system water dependency therefore creates layered relationships across multiple essential systems.

Cross-system water dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2190: Water Dependency Architecture

Water dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, social, agricultural, industrial, and energy systems rely on water resources and supporting infrastructure.

Surface water, groundwater, river basins, transboundary systems, reservoirs, dams, irrigation, urban networks, industry, energy, food systems, desalination, treatment, pumping, distribution, and water technologies form overlapping layers.

Water dependency can be local, regional, transboundary, infrastructural, technological, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within other systems.

The existence of water dependency does not by itself establish scarcity, vulnerability, coercion, political intent, conflict, or a specific political outcome.

Water dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.

The overall water dependency architecture remains observable.
