### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2311: Maritime Route Dependency

Maritime route dependency exists when political, economic, industrial, energy, food, or logistics systems rely on sea routes for movement between production, processing, distribution, and consumption locations.

Maritime routes connect ports, shipping networks, canals, straits, coastal infrastructure, and inland logistics systems.

Dependency can differ by commodity, vessel type, geography, destination, and availability of alternative routes.

Maritime route dependency therefore requires observation of the network rather than sea transport alone.

Maritime route dependency remains observable.

Maritime route dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, industrial, energy, food, and logistics systems rely on shipping lanes, chokepoints, straits, canals, ports, vessels, fleets, fuel, navigation, communications, insurance, workforce, and regulatory systems.
