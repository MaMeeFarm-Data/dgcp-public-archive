### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2431: Technology Import Dependency

Technology import dependency exists when political, economic, industrial, or institutional systems rely on externally supplied technologies for operation, production, communication, computation, or infrastructure.

Imported technology can include hardware, software, machinery, components, digital services, intellectual property, and technical knowledge.

Domestic use or assembly therefore does not necessarily establish domestic control over the complete technology stack.

Technology import dependency requires observation across products, services, knowledge, and supporting infrastructure.

Technology import dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2450: Technology Import Dependency Architecture

Technology import dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, industrial, and institutional systems rely on imported hardware, semiconductors, electronic components, machinery, manufacturing equipment, telecommunications technology, data-center equipment, cloud systems, software, licensing, technical knowledge, maintenance, spare parts, standards, upgrades, and supply networks.

Technology dependency combines industrial, digital, material, legal, institutional, logistical, and human-capability layers.

Dependency can be product-based, component-based, technological, industrial, contractual, knowledge-based, infrastructural, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider systems.

The existence of technology import dependency does not by itself establish vulnerability, technological weakness, political control, coercion, disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Technology import dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.
