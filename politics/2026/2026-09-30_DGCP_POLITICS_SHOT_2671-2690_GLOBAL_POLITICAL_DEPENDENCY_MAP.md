### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2671: Global Political Dependency Map

A global political dependency map describes the structural relationships through which states, institutions, economies, infrastructure systems, industries, and populations rely on capabilities distributed across the Earth system.

Political systems operate within wider material, technological, financial, demographic, informational, institutional, and geographic structures.

Dependency can therefore extend beyond formal political relationships into systems required for continuing economic and institutional activity.

A global political dependency map records these connections without converting structural reliance into claims about control, vulnerability, or future behavior.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2690: Global Political Dependency Architecture

Global political dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which states, institutions, economies, industries, infrastructure systems, and populations rely on resources, energy, food, water, minerals, industrial production, technology, semiconductors, compute, data, communications, transport, logistics, finance, labor, standards, institutions, security relationships, and strategic geography.

These systems do not operate as isolated layers. Resources support production. Production supports infrastructure. Infrastructure supports digital systems. Digital systems support finance, logistics, institutions, and industry. Labor operates across all layers. Standards connect capabilities to wider systems. Geography locates physical dependencies. Political authority provides jurisdictional and institutional interfaces across the structure.

Dependency can be material, technological, infrastructural, financial, demographic, informational, institutional, geographic, security-based, concentrated, distributed, reciprocal, shared, direct, indirect, or embedded within longer dependency chains.

The existence of global political dependency does not by itself establish vulnerability, systemic risk, political control, coercion, hierarchy, strategic weakness, conflict, cascading failure, future disruption, or a specific political outcome.

The global political dependency map therefore records structure rather than prediction and separates observable reliance from interpretations of power, intent, risk, or future behavior.

The overall global political dependency architecture remains observable.
