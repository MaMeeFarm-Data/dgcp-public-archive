### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2051 — Strategic Dependency and Interdependence

Strategic dependency and interdependence connect states through resources, technology, finance, infrastructure, production, trade, information, and institutional systems.

Modern economies operate through networks extending across multiple jurisdictions.

Individual states can simultaneously depend on external capabilities while supplying capabilities required by others.

Dependency and interdependence therefore coexist within regional and global systems.

Strategic dependency and interdependence remain observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2052 — Strategic Dependency

Strategic dependency occurs when an important activity relies substantially on an external resource, capability, supplier, infrastructure system, market, or institution.

Dependencies can exist across economic, technological, industrial, financial, energy, and security systems.

Their structure varies according to concentration, alternatives, substitutability, and access.

Strategic dependency therefore represents a relationship between required capability and external availability.

Strategic dependency remains observable.

I log strategic dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2053 — Strategic Interdependence

Strategic interdependence occurs when states or systems depend on one another through reciprocal or overlapping relationships.

One participant can provide resources, technology, infrastructure, capital, production capacity, or market access while depending on other external capabilities.

Interdependence does not require equal levels of dependency among participants.

Strategic relationships therefore can contain both mutual connection and structural asymmetry.

Strategic interdependence remains observable.

I log strategic interdependence.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2054 — Dependency Mapping

Dependency mapping identifies relationships connecting activities with external resources, suppliers, infrastructure, technologies, markets, or institutions.

Mapping can distinguish direct dependencies from dependencies embedded deeper within supply and infrastructure networks.

Individual systems can contain multiple layers of external reliance.

Dependency mapping therefore supports structural visibility across interconnected systems.

Dependency mapping remains observable.

I log dependency mapping.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2055 — Dependency Concentration

Dependency concentration occurs when reliance is focused on a limited number of suppliers, locations, technologies, infrastructures, markets, or institutions.

Concentration can exist even within otherwise diversified international networks.

The significance of concentration varies according to available alternatives and the function of the dependent capability.

Dependency concentration therefore forms a measurable structural characteristic of interconnected systems.

Dependency concentration remains observable.

I log dependency concentration.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2056 — Asymmetric Interdependence

Asymmetric interdependence exists when interconnected participants experience different levels or forms of dependency.

One participant may possess more alternatives, greater production capacity, wider market access, or stronger control over an important node.

The existence of asymmetry does not by itself establish a specific political outcome.

Asymmetric interdependence therefore requires observation of both connectivity and relative structural positions.

Asymmetric interdependence remains observable.

I log asymmetric interdependence.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2057 — Resource Dependency

Resource dependency connects economies and industries with externally supplied raw materials and natural resources.

Energy resources, minerals, agricultural commodities, water-related systems, and other inputs can move through international networks.

Resource availability depends on production geography, processing, transportation, trade, and infrastructure.

Resource dependency therefore links physical endowments with wider political and economic systems.

Resource dependency remains observable.

I log resource dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2058 — Energy Dependency

Energy dependency emerges when energy consumption relies on external fuels, electricity, infrastructure, technology, or supply networks.

Dependencies can involve oil, natural gas, coal, nuclear fuel, electricity, renewable technologies, or other energy-system components.

Importing, producing, and transit states occupy different positions within energy networks.

Energy dependency therefore connects national systems with regional and global infrastructure.

Energy dependency remains observable.

I log energy dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2059 — Technology Dependency

Technology dependency occurs when important activities rely on technologies, intellectual property, equipment, software, components, or technical capabilities supplied externally.

Dependencies can exist across multiple layers of technology supply chains.

Specialized capabilities may be concentrated among limited companies or jurisdictions.

Technology dependency therefore connects national capacity with internationally distributed innovation and production systems.

Technology dependency remains observable.

I log technology dependency.

---

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2060 — Industrial Dependency

Industrial dependency occurs when domestic production relies on external materials, components, machinery, intermediate goods, technical services, or manufacturing capacity.

Industrial systems can contain dependencies several stages upstream from final production.

Domestic manufacturing therefore can remain connected with extensive international production networks.

Industrial dependency forms part of the structure of modern manufacturing systems.

Industrial dependency remains observable.

I log industrial dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2061 — Infrastructure Dependency

Infrastructure dependency emerges when economic or institutional activity relies on external or cross-border physical systems.

Ports, shipping routes, railways, pipelines, electricity grids, telecommunications networks, data cables, and logistics corridors can create such relationships.

Infrastructure ownership, location, operation, and access can involve different jurisdictions.

Infrastructure dependency therefore connects territorial systems through physical networks.

Infrastructure dependency remains observable.

I log infrastructure dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2062 — Financial Dependency

Financial dependency occurs when governments, institutions, companies, or economic systems rely substantially on external capital, currencies, payment systems, credit, investment, or financial infrastructure.

International financial networks connect borrowers, lenders, investors, banks, markets, and public institutions.

Different participants occupy different positions within these networks.

Financial dependency therefore forms part of wider cross-border economic relationships.

Financial dependency remains observable.

I log financial dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2063 — Market Dependency

Market dependency occurs when producers, industries, or economies rely substantially on particular external markets for sales, inputs, financing, or commercial activity.

Export and import relationships can create different forms of market reliance.

Market concentration and alternative access vary across sectors and products.

Market dependency therefore connects domestic economic activity with external demand and supply systems.

Market dependency remains observable.

I log market dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2064 — Digital Dependency

Digital dependency emerges when institutions, economies, or public systems rely on external digital infrastructure, platforms, software, cloud services, networks, data systems, or technology providers.

Digital services frequently operate through infrastructure distributed across multiple jurisdictions.

Technical ecosystems can contain several layers of provider dependency.

Digital dependency therefore connects national digital systems with global technology networks.

Digital dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2065 — Institutional Dependency

Institutional dependency occurs when activities rely on external organizations, agreements, standards, certification systems, regulatory structures, or international mechanisms.

Institutional relationships can support trade, finance, transportation, technology, communication, and other cross-border systems.

Different institutions provide different forms of coordination and access.

Institutional dependency therefore forms a governance layer within international interdependence.

Institutional dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2066 — Dependency Diversification

Dependency diversification distributes reliance across multiple suppliers, technologies, markets, routes, infrastructures, or institutional relationships.

Diversification can change the concentration of dependency without eliminating external reliance.

Different sectors maintain different possibilities for substitution and alternative access.

Diversification therefore modifies the structure of dependency within interconnected systems.

Dependency diversification remains observable.

I log dependency diversification.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2067 — Substitutability

Substitutability describes the availability of alternative resources, suppliers, technologies, routes, markets, or capabilities within a dependency relationship.

Alternatives can differ in capacity, compatibility, geography, cost, infrastructure, and implementation requirements.

A dependency can therefore have multiple possible substitutes without those substitutes being functionally identical.

Substitutability forms part of the structural assessment of dependency.

Substitutability remains observable.

I log substitutability.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2068 — Strategic Nodes

Strategic nodes are locations, facilities, companies, technologies, institutions, or infrastructures occupying important positions within interconnected systems.

Nodes can connect multiple upstream and downstream relationships.

Their structural significance depends on network position, concentration, capacity, alternatives, and the functions they perform.

Strategic nodes therefore provide identifiable points within dependency and interdependence networks.

Strategic nodes remain observable.

I log strategic nodes.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2069 — Dependency Networks

Dependency networks connect resources, technologies, industries, infrastructure, finance, markets, institutions, and jurisdictions through multiple layers of reliance.

Individual dependencies rarely operate in complete isolation from other systems.

A technology dependency can contain resource, industrial, logistics, energy, financial, and infrastructure dependencies simultaneously.

Network observation therefore reveals relationships that may remain hidden when individual sectors are examined separately.

Dependency networks remain observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2070 — Strategic Dependency and Interdependence Architecture

Strategic dependency and interdependence operate through interconnected systems of resources, energy, technology, industry, infrastructure, finance, markets, digital systems, and institutions.

States and organizations occupy different positions as producers, suppliers, consumers, operators, creditors, markets, and infrastructure nodes.

Dependency can be concentrated, diversified, direct, indirect, reciprocal, or asymmetric depending on the structure of each network.

National capability and external reliance therefore coexist rather than forming completely separate conditions.

The overall strategic dependency and interdependence architecture remains observable.
