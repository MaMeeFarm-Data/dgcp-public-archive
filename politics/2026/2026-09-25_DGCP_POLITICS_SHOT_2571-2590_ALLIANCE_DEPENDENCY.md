### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2571: Alliance Dependency

Alliance dependency exists when political or security systems rely on formal relationships with other states for capabilities, access, coordination, commitments, or institutional support.

Alliance structures can connect national capabilities with resources and systems located beyond national territory.

Membership in an alliance and dependency on particular alliance functions are distinct structural conditions.

Alliance dependency therefore requires observation of the capabilities and relationships beneath formal membership.

Alliance dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2590: Alliance Dependency Architecture

Alliance dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political and security systems rely on commitments, distributed capabilities, interoperability, communications, intelligence, surveillance, logistics, infrastructure, access, basing, procurement, technology, maintenance, training, institutional coordination, and strategic geography.

Alliance systems combine political, institutional, security, industrial, technological, informational, infrastructural, geographic, logistical, and human-capability layers.

Dependency can be capability-based, institutional, technological, geographic, logistical, supplier-based, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider security systems.

The existence of alliance dependency does not by itself establish political control, subordination, loss of sovereignty, coercion, strategic alignment on every issue, alliance effectiveness, future behavior, or a specific political outcome.

Alliance dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.

The overall alliance dependency architecture remains observable.
