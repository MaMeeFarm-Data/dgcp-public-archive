### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2211: Critical Mineral Dependency

Critical mineral dependency exists when political, industrial, technological, energy, or security systems rely on minerals whose supply, processing, refining, or usable production capacity is limited or concentrated.

Mineral dependency can emerge at extraction, processing, refining, chemical conversion, component production, or final industrial-use layers.

Domestic mineral reserves do not necessarily provide domestic access to usable industrial materials.

Critical mineral dependency therefore requires mapping across the complete mineral value chain.

Critical mineral dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2212: Mineral Extraction Dependency

Mineral extraction dependency emerges when industrial systems rely on mined resources originating from a limited set of geological regions or producing jurisdictions.

Ore deposits remain geographically uneven.

Mining capacity also depends on infrastructure, equipment, energy, water, labor, permitting, finance, and technical expertise.

Mineral extraction dependency therefore reflects both geology and the systems required to convert deposits into production.

Mineral extraction dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2213: Mineral Processing Dependency

Mineral processing dependency exists when raw ores or concentrates must pass through specialized processing systems before they become usable industrial inputs.

Processing stages can include crushing, concentration, separation, beneficiation, chemical treatment, or other technical operations.

Processing capacity can be located in jurisdictions different from mining locations.

Mineral processing dependency therefore creates an intermediate layer between extraction and industrial use.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2214: Mineral Refining Dependency

Mineral refining dependency emerges when industrial systems rely on specialized refining capacity to convert processed mineral material into higher-purity products.

Refining requires technology, equipment, energy, chemicals, infrastructure, environmental management, and technical expertise.

Refining capacity can remain more geographically concentrated than raw mineral extraction.

Mineral refining dependency therefore can persist even where raw supply appears geographically diversified.

Processing concentration can create dependency even when geological resources are distributed more widely.

Industrial users may therefore depend on processing nodes located far from extraction sites.

Strategic mineral processing concentration forms an important structural layer within critical mineral systems.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2230: Critical Mineral Dependency Architecture

Critical mineral dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, industrial, technological, energy, and security systems rely on mineral extraction, processing, refining, technology, infrastructure, and international supply networks.

Lithium, cobalt, nickel, copper, rare earth elements, graphite, manganese, platinum group metals, processing technology, energy, water, logistics, and refining capacity form overlapping dependency layers.

Critical mineral dependency can be geological, industrial, technological, infrastructural, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within downstream systems.

The existence of critical mineral dependency does not by itself establish scarcity, vulnerability, coercion, political intent, disruption, or a specific political outcome.

Critical mineral dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.
