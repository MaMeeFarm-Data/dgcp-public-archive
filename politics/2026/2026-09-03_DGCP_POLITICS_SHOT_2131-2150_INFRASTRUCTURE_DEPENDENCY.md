### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2131: Infrastructure Dependency

Infrastructure dependency exists when political, economic, social, or security functions rely on physical and technical systems that enable movement, energy, communication, production, distribution, and essential services.

Infrastructure systems include transport networks, energy systems, telecommunications, water systems, logistics facilities, digital infrastructure, and other supporting networks.

These systems frequently connect multiple sectors and jurisdictions.

Infrastructure dependency therefore reflects relationships between essential functions and the systems required to sustain them.

Infrastructure dependency remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2150: Infrastructure Dependency Architecture

Infrastructure dependency architecture describes the interconnected physical and technical systems on which political, economic, social, industrial, digital, and security functions rely.

Transportation, ports, airports, railways, roads, energy systems, electricity grids, pipelines, water infrastructure, telecommunications, subsea cables, data centers, satellite ground systems, logistics hubs, and urban networks form overlapping layers.

Dependencies can be domestic, cross-border, distributed, concentrated, direct, or embedded within wider systems.

Infrastructure dependence does not by itself establish vulnerability, political leverage, coercion, failure, or a specific outcome.

Infrastructure dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.
