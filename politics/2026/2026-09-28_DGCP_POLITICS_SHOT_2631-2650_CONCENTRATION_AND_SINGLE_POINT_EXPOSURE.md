### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2631: Concentration & Single-Point Exposure

Concentration and single-point exposure exists when multiple activities, capabilities, or systems rely on a limited set of common resources, providers, facilities, routes, technologies, or institutions.

A system can contain many visible participants while retaining concentrated dependencies beneath them.

The number of organizations or assets therefore does not by itself establish structural diversification.

Concentration and single-point exposure requires observation of the underlying capabilities shared across systems.

Concentration and single-point exposure remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2648: Single-Point Exposure

Single-point exposure occurs when a system function depends on a particular node, facility, route, provider, technology, resource, institution, or capability for which equivalent substitution is not simultaneously present within the mapped structure.

A single point can exist within otherwise large and distributed systems.

Its presence describes structural architecture rather than the probability or consequence of disruption.

Single-point exposure therefore must remain distinct from claims of failure, vulnerability, or future system behavior.

Single-point exposure remains observable.

I log single-point exposure.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2650: Concentration & Single-Point Exposure Architecture

Concentration and single-point exposure architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, industrial, infrastructure, technological, and security systems rely on concentrated suppliers, production capacity, facilities, infrastructure nodes, routes, geography, resources, processing, technology, equipment, digital infrastructure, financial infrastructure, standards, knowledge, skills, and institutions.

Concentration can exist at one layer while diversification exists at another, and apparently independent systems can retain hidden common dependencies beneath visible structures.

Exposure can be supplier-based, facility-based, route-based, geographic, technological, institutional, capability-based, concentrated, shared, direct, or embedded across multiple systems.

The existence of concentration or a single-point exposure does not by itself establish vulnerability, imminent disruption, market power, political control, coercion, strategic weakness, system failure, or a specific political outcome.

Concentration and single-point exposure architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural concentration from interpretation.

The overall concentration and single-point exposure architecture remains observable.
