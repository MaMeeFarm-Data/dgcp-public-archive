### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2491: Migration Dependency

Migration dependency exists when political, economic, industrial, or social systems rely on the movement of people between regions or jurisdictions for labor, skills, services, demographic support, or other functions.

Migration can be internal or cross-border, temporary or long-term, seasonal or continuous.

The presence of migration alone does not establish dependency.

Migration dependency requires observation of which systems rely on particular flows of people and the functions those flows support.

Migration dependency remains observable.

I log migration dependency.

Migrant labor dependency emerges when industries, services, households, or institutions rely on workers who have moved from another region or jurisdiction.

Migrant workers can participate across agriculture, construction, manufacturing, logistics, care, hospitality, technology, and other sectors.

Dependency can differ substantially across occupations and locations.

Migrant labor dependency therefore requires sector-specific and geographic observation.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2508: Migration Concentration

Migration concentration occurs when particular industries, regions, cities, occupations, or services rely heavily on migrant populations from a limited number of origins or movement channels.

National migration totals can conceal substantial sectoral and geographic concentration.

Different employers can also depend on common recruitment networks, border routes, or worker communities.

Migration concentration therefore requires observation below aggregate population figures.

Migration concentration remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2509: Cross-System Migration Dependency

Cross-system migration dependency occurs when population movement relies simultaneously on labor markets, transport, housing, recruitment, documentation, legal systems, finance, education, healthcare, communications, and administrative infrastructure.

Agriculture, industry, construction, care, technology, services, and households can in turn rely on migrant workers or specialized skills.

Dependency therefore operates both beneath migration systems and outward into economic and social systems supported by mobile populations.

Cross-system mapping reveals these relationships without establishing migration pressure, social instability, political intent, exploitation, demographic outcome, or policy effect.

Cross-system migration dependency remains observable.

I log cross-system migration dependency.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2510: Migration Dependency Architecture

Migration dependency architecture describes the interconnected structure through which political, economic, industrial, and social systems rely on migrant labor, cross-border movement, internal migration, seasonal mobility, agricultural workers, construction workers, industrial workers, care workers, skilled migration, recruitment, documentation, legal status, transport, housing, remittances, and migration corridors.

Migration systems combine demographic, economic, geographic, institutional, legal, financial, transport, housing, and human-capability layers.

Dependency can be labor-based, skill-based, demographic, geographic, institutional, jurisdictional, seasonal, concentrated, distributed, direct, or embedded within wider economic and social systems.

The existence of migration dependency does not by itself establish social benefit or harm, demographic weakness, political control, exploitation, instability, policy success or failure, or a specific political outcome.

Migration dependency architecture therefore requires continuous separation of observable structural reliance from interpretation.

The overall migration dependency architecture remains observable.
