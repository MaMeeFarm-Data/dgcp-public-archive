### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2024 — Information Verification

Information verification examines evidence supporting factual claims within information environments.

Verification can involve source assessment, documents, data, images, video, timestamps, institutional records, and independent corroboration.

Media organizations, researchers, institutions, platforms, and individuals use different verification processes.

Verification therefore forms a distinct layer between information availability and evidentiary confidence.

Information verification remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2025 — Source Provenance

Source provenance records the origin and transmission history of information or evidence.

Provenance can identify who produced material, when it was created, where it originated, and how it moved between systems.

Digital information can be copied, modified, redistributed, or separated from its original context.

Provenance therefore supports structural understanding of information chains.

Source provenance remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2029 — Narrative Interdependence

Narrative interdependence emerges when political narratives circulate across borders, platforms, media systems, institutions, and communities.

Narratives originating within one information environment can be repeated, contested, reframed, or incorporated elsewhere.

Domestic political communication therefore interacts continuously with international information flows.

Narrative competition and information connectivity coexist within these systems.

Narrative interdependence remains observable.

### DGCP™ Politics Shot #2030 — Information and Narrative Competition Architecture

Information and narrative competition operates through interconnected systems of government communication, public diplomacy, media, broadcasting, digital platforms, social networks, algorithms, verification, provenance, and cross-border information flows.

Governments, media organizations, technology companies, institutions, civil society, and individuals occupy different positions within these information networks.

Information availability does not by itself establish accuracy, and narrative prevalence does not by itself establish evidentiary validity.

Communication, competition, verification, dependency, and interdependence therefore coexist within the global information environment.

The overall information and narrative competition architecture remains observable.
