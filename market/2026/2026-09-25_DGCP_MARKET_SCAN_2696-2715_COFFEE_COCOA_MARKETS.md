### DGCP™ Market Scan #2696: Coffee and Cocoa Markets

Coffee and cocoa markets connect perennial crops, agricultural land, climate, water, labor, harvesting, processing, storage, transportation, trade, manufacturing, and consumer markets.

Both commodities originate from biological production concentrated within particular growing environments before moving through international processing and distribution systems.

The markets therefore connect agricultural landscapes with global food and beverage industries.

I don't see these commodities operating through agriculture alone.

I log coffee and cocoa markets.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2697: Coffee Production Systems

Coffee production connects perennial plants with land, climate, water, nutrients, farm management, labor, harvesting, and post harvest processing.

Production systems differ by coffee species, geography, altitude, farm structure, cultivation practices, and processing access.

Coffee supply therefore originates from differentiated biological and agricultural systems.

I don't see coffee production becoming structurally uniform.

Coffee production is geographically concentrated in regions where environmental conditions support commercial cultivation.

Growing regions differ in elevation, rainfall, temperature, soils, infrastructure, labor systems, and access to processing and export networks.

Global coffee supply therefore depends on geographically differentiated production landscapes.

I don't see production geography becoming irrelevant.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2699: Coffee Harvest Systems

Coffee harvesting converts mature coffee fruit into collected agricultural material ready for post harvest processing.

Harvest systems differ in timing, labor intensity, mechanization, crop characteristics, farm structure, and local operating conditions.

Biological production therefore requires a distinct collection stage before coffee enters commodity supply chains.

I don't see harvesting becoming structurally secondary.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2700: Coffee Processing

Coffee processing transforms harvested coffee fruit into material suitable for drying, storage, grading, export, roasting, and consumption.

Processing pathways involve different combinations of fruit removal, fermentation, washing, drying, hulling, and preparation.

The physical characteristics of traded coffee therefore emerge partly through post harvest transformation.

I don't see processing becoming structurally optional.

I log coffee processing.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2701: Green Coffee

Green coffee forms the major intermediate commodity connecting producing regions with international traders, roasters, manufacturers, and consuming markets.

Its commercial characteristics depend on origin, species, quality, processing method, grading, storage, and physical condition.

Green coffee therefore creates a distinct market layer between farms and finished beverage products.

I don't see that intermediate layer becoming irrelevant.

Downstream systems also include grinding, blending, extraction, soluble coffee production, packaging, and other processing pathways.

Consumer coffee supply therefore depends on industrial transformation after agricultural production and international trade.
