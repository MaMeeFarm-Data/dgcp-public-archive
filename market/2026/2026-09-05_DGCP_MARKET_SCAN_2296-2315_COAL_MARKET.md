### DGCP™ Market Scan #2297: Coal Resources

Coal resources occur across geological formations with different grades, depths, locations, and extraction conditions.

Commercial accessibility depends on geology, mining methods, infrastructure, capital, regulation, and operating conditions.

Resource presence does not automatically translate into marketable supply.

I don’t see resource conditions becoming uniform.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2298: Coal Reserves

Coal reserves represent identified resources considered recoverable under defined technical and economic conditions.

Reserve classifications interact with geological knowledge, extraction capability, infrastructure, costs, and commercial viability.

Reported reserves remain distinct from immediate production and delivery capacity.

I don’t see that distinction disappearing.

Coal mining converts geological resources into physical commodity supply.

Production depends on mine development, equipment, workforce, energy, water, transport access, operating capability, and geological conditions.

Mining output remains distributed across different resource basins and production systems.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2300: Surface Coal Mining

Surface coal mining extracts deposits located close enough to the surface for open pit or strip mining methods.

Operations depend on excavation equipment, overburden removal, haulage, land access, workforce, and supporting infrastructure.

Surface mining creates operating structures distinct from underground production.

I don’t see those structural differences disappearing.

I log surface coal mining.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2301: Underground Coal Mining

Underground coal mining accesses deposits beneath the surface through shafts, tunnels, and specialized extraction systems.

Operations depend on ventilation, ground control, machinery, transport, safety systems, workforce, and continuous infrastructure.

Underground production therefore carries different physical and operational requirements from surface mining.

I don’t see those distinctions fading.

I log underground coal mining.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2302: Thermal Coal

Thermal coal is primarily used as a fuel for electricity generation and industrial heat.

Its market connects mines, power systems, industrial users, transport networks, storage, and trade.

Coal quality and plant requirements influence where different grades can be consumed.

I don’t see thermal coal becoming a structurally isolated commodity.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2303: Metallurgical Coal

Metallurgical coal is used within steelmaking processes where specific coal properties are required.

Its market connects mining with coke production, blast furnace operations, steel plants, logistics, and international trade.

Quality requirements create a market structure distinct from many thermal coal applications.

I don’t see those specifications becoming irrelevant.

I log metallurgical coal.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2304: Coal Quality

Coal quality varies by energy content, moisture, ash, sulfur, volatile matter, coking characteristics, and other physical properties.

These differences influence processing, transport economics, plant compatibility, emissions profiles, and end use.

Coal is therefore not a fully interchangeable commodity across all markets.

I don’t see quality distinctions disappearing.

I log coal quality.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2305: Coal Processing

Coal processing prepares mined material for transportation, trade, or downstream use.

Processing can include crushing, sizing, washing, blending, dewatering, and removal of unwanted material.

This layer connects mine output with product specifications required by customers and logistics systems.

I don’t see processing becoming structurally secondary.

I log coal processing.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2306: Coal Transportation

Coal transportation connects mines with processing facilities, storage sites, ports, power plants, steel mills, and industrial users.

Rail, road, conveyor, river, and maritime systems move coal across different distances and geographic environments.

Transport availability influences whether mined coal can reach commercial demand.

I don’t see logistics losing structural importance.

I log coal transportation.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2307: Coal Rail Networks

Rail networks move large coal volumes between mining regions, inland demand centers, and export terminals.

Rail capacity depends on track infrastructure, locomotives, wagons, loading systems, scheduling, maintenance, and route availability.

Mine production can therefore remain constrained by transport capacity beyond the mine itself.

I don’t see rail connectivity becoming secondary.

I log coal rail networks.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2308: Coal Export Terminals

Coal export terminals connect inland mining and transportation systems with international maritime trade.

Terminal operations depend on rail or conveyor access, stockyards, handling equipment, berths, vessel loading systems, and port capacity.

Export capability therefore forms a distinct layer between production and seaborne markets.

I don’t see terminal capacity losing relevance.

I log coal export terminals.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2309: Coal Shipping

Coal shipping connects export terminals with power, steel, and industrial markets across maritime trade routes.

Seaborne flows depend on vessel availability, port access, cargo handling, route geography, freight conditions, and destination infrastructure.

Maritime transport remains a major component of international coal trade.

I don’t see shipping becoming structurally irrelevant.

Stockpiles help coordinate supply systems with changing transport and consumption patterns.
