### DGCP™ Market Scan #2217: Crude Oil Resources

Crude oil resources exist across geological formations with different volumes, qualities, extraction conditions, and development requirements.

Commercial accessibility depends on geology, technology, infrastructure, capital, and operating conditions.

Resource existence does not automatically translate into usable market supply.

I don’t see resource conditions becoming uniform.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2218: Oil Reserves

Oil reserves represent identified petroleum resources considered recoverable under defined technical and economic conditions.

Reserve classifications depend on geological knowledge, development capability, operating assumptions, and commercial viability.

Reported reserves remain distinct from immediate production capacity.

I don’t see that distinction disappearing.

Output remains distributed across different producers and geographic regions.

I don’t see production becoming structurally independent.

Their geographic structure influences how physical supply can reach markets.

I don’t see pipeline geography losing relevance.

Crude oil tankers move petroleum between maritime production regions, export terminals, storage centers, and refining markets.

Shipping capacity interacts with vessel availability, port infrastructure, maritime routes, freight conditions, and trade flows.

Seaborne transport remains a major link within global oil circulation.

Oil export terminals connect inland production and storage systems with international maritime trade.

Terminal operations depend on pipelines, storage tanks, loading infrastructure, ports, vessels, and operational coordination.

Crude oil storage provides physical buffering between production, transportation, refining, and consumption.

Storage facilities operate across production areas, transport hubs, terminals, refineries, and commercial centers.

Available storage affects how physical supply can be managed across time and location.

I don’t see storage losing its system role.

Strategic petroleum reserves maintain crude oil or petroleum inventories outside ordinary commercial operating flows.

These reserves connect energy security structures with storage infrastructure, logistics, government authority, and market systems.

Their function differs from normal commercial inventories.

I don’t see that institutional distinction fading.

Crude oil trade flows connect producing regions with refining and consuming markets across geographic boundaries.

Physical flows respond to resource location, crude quality, transport infrastructure, refinery requirements, contracts, and commercial relationships.

Crude oil pricing connects physical grades, benchmark markets, transportation costs, location, quality, inventories, and commercial expectations.

Oil benchmark markets provide reference prices used across physical contracts, financial instruments, and commercial transactions.
