### DGCP™ Market Scan #2756: Water as an Economic Resource

Water systems connect natural hydrology, infrastructure, agriculture, energy, industry, cities, households, ecosystems, and economic activity.

Physical water availability alone does not determine whether water can be captured, treated, transported, stored, allocated, and used.

Water therefore operates simultaneously as a natural resource and an input to multiple economic systems.

I don't see water functioning through one resource layer alone.

I log water as an economic resource.

---

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2757: Freshwater Resources

Freshwater resources exist across rivers, lakes, reservoirs, groundwater systems, glaciers, soil moisture, and other hydrological structures.

Their accessibility differs according to geography, season, quality, infrastructure, depth, flow, storage, and competing uses.

Aggregate freshwater volume therefore does not represent uniformly usable supply.

I don't see freshwater availability becoming structurally uniform.

I log freshwater resources.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2758: Surface Water Systems

Surface water systems include rivers, lakes, reservoirs, canals, wetlands, and other above ground water bodies and flows.

These systems connect natural hydrology with agriculture, cities, industry, energy, transportation, and ecosystems.

Economic access depends on location, flow conditions, infrastructure, storage, quality, and allocation.

I don't see surface water functioning independently of surrounding systems.

I log surface water systems.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2759: Groundwater Systems

Groundwater systems store water below the surface within aquifers and geological formations.

Economic use depends on aquifer characteristics, well access, pumping capacity, energy, water quality, recharge, infrastructure, and location.

Physical groundwater presence therefore does not automatically equal accessible water supply.

I don't see groundwater availability becoming structurally uniform.

I log groundwater systems.

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2760: Water Capture Infrastructure

Water capture infrastructure connects natural water flows with human use through dams, reservoirs, intakes, wells, diversion structures, and related systems.

Infrastructure determines how portions of naturally available water can enter managed supply networks.

Resource availability therefore differs from operational capture capacity.

I don't see that distinction disappearing.

I log water capture infrastructure.

---

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2761: Water Storage

Water storage shifts availability across time by holding water between periods of inflow and periods of use.

Reservoirs, tanks, ponds, aquifers, and other storage systems operate at different geographic and economic scales.

Stored volume alone does not describe location, quality, ownership, accessibility, or delivery capacity.

I don't see storage becoming structurally secondary.

I log water storage.

---

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2762: Water Treatment

Water treatment changes raw water characteristics to meet requirements for drinking, industrial processing, agriculture, manufacturing, or other uses.

Treatment depends on source quality, technology, chemicals, energy, facilities, monitoring, and operating capacity.

Usable water supply therefore depends on quality transformation as well as physical availability.

I don't see treatment becoming structurally optional.

I log water treatment.

---

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2763: Water Distribution Networks

Water distribution networks move treated or untreated water between sources, storage facilities, users, and economic activities.

Pipelines, pumps, canals, pressure systems, control infrastructure, and local networks determine physical delivery capacity.

Water existing within a region does not automatically mean it can reach every user.

I don't see distribution infrastructure becoming structurally irrelevant.

I log water distribution networks.

---

### DGCP™ Market Scan #2764: Irrigation Systems

Irrigation systems connect water resources with agricultural land through reservoirs, canals, pumps, pipes, wells, control structures, and field level delivery.

Their operation links hydrology with energy, infrastructure, crop systems, farming practices, and seasonal demand.

Agricultural water availability therefore depends on delivery capability as well as water resources.

I don't see irrigation becoming structurally separate from food production.

I log irrigation systems.
