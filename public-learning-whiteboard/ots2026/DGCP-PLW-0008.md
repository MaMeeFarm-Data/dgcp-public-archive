## Purpose

This public learning whiteboard explains the characteristics that make evidence useful for understanding reality.

The objective is to help learners evaluate evidence by considering its relevance, accuracy, reliability, verifiability, completeness, timeliness, and context.

## Relevant

Evidence should relate directly to the question, issue, or phenomenon being examined.

Information may be accurate while still being irrelevant to the question under investigation.

---

## Accurate

Evidence should represent what occurred or was measured as closely as the available method allows.

Accuracy depends on the quality of observation, measurement, recording, and documentation.

---

## Reliable

Reliable evidence comes from sources or methods that can reasonably be depended upon for the intended purpose.

Reliability should be evaluated rather than assumed.

---

## Verifiable

Evidence should be capable of being checked, tested, compared, or independently confirmed where possible.

Verifiability allows others to examine the basis of a claim.

---

## Complete (Enough)

Evidence should provide sufficient information for the question, context, and intended purpose.

Completeness does not necessarily mean recording everything.

It means having enough relevant information to support responsible interpretation.

---

## Timely

Evidence should be current enough for the question being examined.

The usefulness of evidence may change as conditions, systems, or circumstances change over time.

---

## Examples of Evidence Quality

Examples of stronger evidence may include:

- Photographs with relevant timestamp and location information
- Official records from verifiable sources
- Measurements from appropriately calibrated instruments
- Multiple independent observations or witnesses

Examples of weaker evidence may include:

- Personal opinion presented as fact
- Rumors or hearsay
- Information without necessary context
- Single unverified statements

Evidence quality should always be evaluated according to the question and context.

---

## Sources of Evidence

Evidence may come from:

- Direct Observation
- Measurement
- Official Records
- Expert Testimony
- Data Logs
- Research Studies

Different sources provide different forms and strengths of evidence.

No source should automatically be treated as sufficient without considering its quality, relevance, and context.

---

## Key Idea

Good evidence reduces uncertainty.

Good evidence strengthens analysis.

Good evidence contributes to better understanding.

Better understanding supports better-informed decisions and responsible actions.

---

A simple evidence process is:

↓

↓

## Key Message

Evidence is a building block of knowledge.

The existence of evidence alone is not enough.

Its quality, relevance, reliability, verifiability, completeness, timeliness, and context determine how useful it is for understanding reality.
