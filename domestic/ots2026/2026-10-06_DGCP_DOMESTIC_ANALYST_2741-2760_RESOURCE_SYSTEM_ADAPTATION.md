### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2741: Resource Availability to System Usability

Domestic resource systems depend on more than the physical presence of energy, minerals, water, land, or biological resources.

Extraction, treatment, processing, transportation, storage, infrastructure, labor, and market access determine whether available resources can enter productive systems.

Changes in any of these interfaces can alter the usable capacity of an existing resource base without changing the underlying physical resource itself.

The distinction between resource availability and system usability remains observable.

I log resource availability to system usability.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2742: Resource Extraction Capacity Adaptation

Extraction systems operate through equipment, energy, labor, water, logistics, technical services, and supporting infrastructure.

When operating conditions change, resource production can be adjusted through different combinations of equipment utilization, maintenance, processing, sequencing, and supply support.

Adaptation therefore occurs partly through the organization of extraction capacity rather than through discovery of additional resources.

Resource-extraction capacity adaptation remains observable.

I log resource extraction capacity adaptation.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2743: Resource Processing Reconfiguration

Raw resources frequently require processing before they can enter industrial, energy, food, or commercial systems.

Processing plants connect resource supply with electricity, water, equipment, labor, transportation, storage, and downstream users.

Changes in processing location, utilization, technology, or product mix can reorganize how an existing resource base moves through the domestic economy.

Resource-processing reconfiguration remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2744: Energy Source Substitution Interface

Domestic energy systems can contain multiple sources including hydrocarbons, hydropower, solar, wind, biomass, geothermal resources, and imported electricity.

Different energy sources require different generation, storage, transmission, fuel, land, and operating infrastructure.

Changes in the relative use of these sources alter the physical configuration connecting energy resources with domestic demand.

The energy-source substitution interface remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2745: Fuel Supply System Adaptation

Fuel systems connect production or import gateways with refineries, terminals, storage, pipelines, trucks, distributors, power systems, industry, and transportation.

Changes in supply routes or availability can shift activity among storage facilities, transport modes, suppliers, and consumption points.

Domestic fuel adaptation therefore occurs through logistics and distribution systems as well as through changes in total supply.

Fuel-supply system adaptation remains observable.

I log fuel supply system adaptation.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2746: Electricity Generation Mix Adaptation

Electricity systems combine generation resources with transmission, distribution, balancing capacity, storage, and demand.

Changes in fuel availability, hydrological conditions, renewable output, plant utilization, or imported electricity can alter the generation mix.

The grid provides the physical interface through which different energy sources are combined into a common domestic electricity system.

Electricity-generation mix adaptation remains observable.

I log electricity generation mix adaptation.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2747: Water Allocation System Adaptation

Water resources connect households, agriculture, industry, energy systems, ecosystems, and public infrastructure.

Reservoirs, rivers, groundwater, treatment plants, pipelines, irrigation networks, and distribution systems determine how available water reaches competing uses.

Changes in allocation or infrastructure utilization can reorganize domestic water flows without changing the geographic location of the underlying resource.

Water-allocation system adaptation remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2748: Agricultural Land Use Reconfiguration

Agricultural land connects soil, water, crops, livestock, labor, machinery, inputs, transportation, and markets.

Changes in crop selection, irrigation, production intensity, land use, or market orientation can alter how existing agricultural resources are utilized.

This reconfiguration occurs within the physical limits created by land quality, water availability, climate, infrastructure, and access to inputs.

Agricultural land-use reconfiguration remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2749: Food Production System Adaptation

Domestic food systems combine agricultural production, fisheries, livestock, processing, storage, transportation, imports, distribution, and retail.

Changes in one supply component can shift activity toward other production sources, commodities, regions, or distribution channels.

Food-system adaptation therefore occurs across multiple connected layers rather than exclusively at the farm level.

Food-production system adaptation remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2750: Mineral Resource Utilization Adaptation

Mineral resources connect extraction sites with processing plants, electricity, water, transportation, industrial users, storage, and export gateways.

Changes in downstream processing or domestic industrial demand can alter where value-chain activity occurs after extraction.

The same geological resource base can therefore support different physical production structures depending on processing and infrastructure capacity.

Mineral-resource utilization adaptation remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2751: Critical Material Supply Interface

Industrial systems depend on materials that differ substantially in geological availability, processing complexity, production concentration, and logistics requirements.

Domestic access can involve local extraction, processing, recycling, inventories, imports, or combinations of these sources.

The usable material system is therefore shaped by both resource geography and the infrastructure connecting materials with production.

Critical-material supply adaptation remains observable.

I log the critical material supply interface.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2752: Domestic Processing Capacity Expansion Interface

Resource-producing systems can export raw materials or connect them with additional domestic processing stages.

Processing requires energy, water, equipment, industrial sites, technical labor, transportation, financing, and market access.

The addition or utilization of processing capacity changes the domestic relationship between resource extraction and downstream production.

Domestic processing-capacity adaptation remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2753: Resource Storage System Adaptation

Storage separates the timing of resource supply from the timing of consumption or onward movement.

Fuel tanks, grain silos, reservoirs, mineral stockpiles, warehouses, cold storage, and other facilities perform different buffering functions.

Changes in storage capacity or inventory location can alter how domestic systems absorb interruptions and variations in physical flows.

Resource-storage system adaptation remains observable.

I log resource storage system adaptation.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2754: Resource Transport Mode Reconfiguration

Resources move through pipelines, railways, roads, waterways, ports, transmission lines, and other specialized networks.

Different transport modes have distinct capacity, geography, infrastructure, and handling requirements.

Changes in route or mode can reorganize the physical connection between resource-producing areas and processing, consumption, or export locations.

Resource-transport mode reconfiguration remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2755: Import Dependency Rebalancing Interface

Domestic systems frequently combine locally produced resources with imported energy, food, materials, equipment, or industrial inputs.

Changes in domestic production, processing, storage, efficiency, substitution, or external sourcing can alter the composition of supply.

Import dependency therefore operates as a relationship between domestic capacity and external resource networks rather than as an isolated trade measure.

The import-dependency rebalancing interface remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2756: Resource Recycling & Secondary Supply

Secondary materials can return previously used resources to productive systems through collection, sorting, recovery, processing, and reuse.

Recycling infrastructure connects households, businesses, waste systems, industrial facilities, transportation, and material markets.

This creates an additional supply layer alongside primary extraction and imports.

Resource recycling and secondary-supply adaptation remains observable.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2757: Resource Efficiency & Production Interface

The quantity of resource input required for production depends partly on technology, equipment, process design, maintenance, and operating practices.

Changes in these systems can alter the relationship between physical resource consumption and productive output.

Resource adaptation can therefore occur through changes in utilization as well as through changes in supply.

Resource systems frequently share geographic space with energy, water, transport, processing, storage, and industrial infrastructure.

Co-location can connect mines with power systems, agricultural areas with irrigation, ports with processing facilities, or energy resources with industrial users.

Changes in infrastructure concentration can reorganize the distance and interfaces between resource supply and productive capacity.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2759: Resource System Dependency Mapping

Domestic production systems depend on interconnected flows of energy, water, food, minerals, materials, logistics, labor, and infrastructure.

A resource system can therefore depend on other resources before its own output becomes usable.

These cross-resource relationships create dependencies that extend beyond individual commodity categories.

### DGCP™ Domestic Analyst #2760: Resource System Adaptation Structure

Resource adaptation occurs across extraction, processing, energy, water, agriculture, storage, transportation, recycling, substitution, and domestic production systems.

The underlying resource base represents only one layer of usable national capacity.

Infrastructure, processing, logistics, technology, institutions, and downstream demand determine how physical resources are converted into functioning economic inputs.

The reconfiguration of relationships between resources and domestic capacity remains observable.
