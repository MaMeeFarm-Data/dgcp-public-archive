Change over time describes how a system, state, relationship, flow, or measurable condition differs across successive observations.

A single observation shows a condition at one point in time.

Multiple observations make change visible.

Change may appear as increase, decrease, fluctuation, persistence, accumulation, decline, recovery, or transition between observed conditions.

Observation records what exists at each point in time.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding each observation.

Time establishes sequence.

Comparing observations across time helps identify what changed, when the change became visible, and whether the observed behavior persisted or varied.

The rate and direction of change may differ across periods.

Some changes may appear quickly.

Others may become visible only after repeated observation.

A change observed after another event does not by itself establish that the earlier event caused it.

Repeated change does not by itself establish that the same pattern will continue.

Recording observations with consistent timing, relevant conditions, system state, and available evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish observed change from interpretation.

---

## Principle

Observe the condition.

Record the time.

Compare successive states.

Identify what changed.

Preserve the relevant context.

Separate sequence from causality.

Do not convert persistence into prediction.

Maintain traceability.

Review change as new observations become available.
