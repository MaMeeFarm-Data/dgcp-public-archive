System integrity describes the condition in which a system maintains its defined structure, relationships, state, data, functions, and operating boundaries without unintended or unverified alteration.

Integrity may involve the consistency of data, configuration, interfaces, dependencies, processes, controls, records, and other elements required for the system to operate as defined.

Identifying system integrity helps distinguish verified system conditions from conditions in which components, relationships, data, or behavior have changed unexpectedly or cannot be adequately verified.

Observation identifies the system condition and behavior that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding observed changes.

System state provides information about conditions before, during, and after an event or change.

Boundaries define the scope within which integrity is being examined.

Interfaces and dependencies help identify where changes may enter, propagate, or affect connected parts of the system.

Controls may restrict, detect, or record changes to defined system elements.

Traceability helps connect observed changes with relevant events, actions, evidence, and system states.

Integrity does not mean that a system cannot change.

Authorized, intentional, and verified changes may occur while system integrity is maintained.

An unexplained difference does not by itself establish loss of integrity. The difference requires investigation, evidence, and verification within the relevant scope.

Verification helps determine whether the system remains consistent with its defined state, structure, rules, and expected relationships after a change or event.

Recording system states, changes, responsible actions, interfaces, dependencies, relevant evidence, and verification results supports traceability and helps distinguish controlled change from unintended or unverified alteration.

---

## Principle

Define the system scope.

Establish the relevant state.

Observe changes.

Preserve the evidence and context.

Trace affected components and relationships.

Verify authorized and expected changes.

Investigate unexplained differences.

Confirm the resulting state.

Maintain traceability.

Review integrity when the system or its operating conditions change.
