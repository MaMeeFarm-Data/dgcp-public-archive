Encapsulation is the organization of related information, functions, or state within a defined boundary while controlling how they are accessed or changed from outside that boundary.

A component may expose defined interfaces while keeping internal structure, implementation details, or state management within its boundary.

Identifying what is contained and what is exposed helps clarify responsibilities, interactions, and dependencies between connected parts of a system.

Observation identifies what exists within and across the boundary.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain why particular information or functions are contained or exposed.

Encapsulation separates internal implementation from defined external interaction.

Defined interfaces specify how other components or systems may interact with the encapsulated component.

Changes within the boundary may occur without requiring changes outside the boundary when the defined interface and expected behavior remain compatible.

Encapsulation does not remove dependencies or interactions.

Recording boundaries, exposed interfaces, relevant dependencies, and changes supports traceability and helps distinguish internal implementation from external interaction.

---

## Principle

Define the boundary.

Identify what is contained.

Identify what is exposed.

Define the interface.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review encapsulation when boundaries, interfaces, dependencies, or responsibilities change.
