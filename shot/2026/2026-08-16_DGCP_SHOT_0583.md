Layered architecture is the organization of a system into defined layers, with each layer responsible for a particular set of functions or concerns.

A layer may provide services, information, or capabilities to another layer through defined interfaces.

Identifying layers helps clarify responsibilities, dependencies, interfaces, and the movement of information or control across a system.

Observation identifies the components, functions, and relationships that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain why particular responsibilities are assigned to particular layers.

Layers do not imply complete independence.

A layer may depend on capabilities provided by other layers and may support functions performed elsewhere in the system.

Defined interfaces help clarify how layers interact and what each layer is expected to provide or receive.

Changes within one layer may have limited effects on other layers when responsibilities, dependencies, and interfaces are clearly defined and maintained.

The appropriate number, purpose, and arrangement of layers depend on system requirements, structure, dependencies, and operating conditions.

Recording layers, responsibilities, interfaces, dependencies, and relevant changes supports traceability and helps distinguish the internal organization of the system from the interactions between its layers.

---

## Principle

Identify the responsibilities.

Define the layers.

Define the interfaces.

Map the dependencies.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the architecture when responsibilities, interfaces, dependencies, or system conditions change.
