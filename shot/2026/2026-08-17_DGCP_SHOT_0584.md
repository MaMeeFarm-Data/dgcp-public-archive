Service boundaries define the scope within which a service performs its responsibilities and the interfaces through which it interacts with other parts of a system.

A service boundary may distinguish responsibilities, data ownership, operations, dependencies, interfaces, and conditions of interaction.

Identifying service boundaries helps clarify what a service is responsible for, what remains outside its scope, and where dependencies or interactions occur.

Observation identifies the functions, responsibilities, and relationships that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain why particular responsibilities are placed within or outside a service boundary.

A service boundary does not imply complete independence.

Services may depend on information, capabilities, infrastructure, or outputs provided by other services or systems.

Defined interfaces help clarify what crosses a boundary and the conditions under which interaction occurs.

Changes within a service may have limited effects outside its boundary when responsibilities, dependencies, interfaces, and expected behavior remain clearly defined and compatible.

The appropriate boundary depends on system purpose, responsibilities, dependencies, data relationships, and operating conditions.

Recording service boundaries, responsibilities, interfaces, dependencies, and relevant changes supports traceability and helps distinguish internal service responsibilities from interactions across the wider system.

---

## Principle

Identify the responsibility.

Define the service boundary.

Identify what belongs inside and outside.

Define the interfaces.

Map the dependencies.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the boundary when responsibilities, interfaces, dependencies, or system conditions change.
