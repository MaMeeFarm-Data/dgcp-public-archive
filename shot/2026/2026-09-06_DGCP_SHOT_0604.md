Delays describe the time between a change, event, input, action, or condition and an observable response elsewhere in a system.

A delay may occur in information transfer, processing, resource movement, decision execution, feedback, recovery, adaptation, or other system interactions.

Identifying delays helps distinguish when something occurs from when its effects or responses become observable.

Observation identifies the events, conditions, and responses that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the observed timing.

Time establishes sequence and allows intervals between observations to be recorded.

A system may continue to reflect an earlier condition while information, resources, actions, or effects move through delayed processes.

Different parts of the same system may respond at different times.

Delays may also affect feedback relationships by separating a change from the information or response associated with it.

An observed time interval does not by itself establish why the delay occurred.

A response occurring after an event does not by itself establish that the event caused the response.

Delay duration may vary when system conditions, capacity, dependencies, constraints, or flows change.

Recording relevant events, timestamps, state changes, responses, conditions, and available evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish observed timing from assumptions about mechanism.

---

## Principle

Identify the relevant events or conditions.

Record when each is observed.

Measure the observable interval where possible.

Preserve the system state and context.

Distinguish sequence from causality.

Do not assume the cause of a delay without evidence.

Maintain traceability.

Review timing as system conditions change.
