Decision points are defined moments or conditions at which a choice between available actions, paths, or responses is required within a system or process.

A decision point may arise from an observed condition, threshold, event, trigger, constraint, change in state, new evidence, or another defined system condition.

Identifying decision points helps clarify when a decision is required, what information is available, which alternatives exist, and what conditions influence the choice.

Observation identifies the conditions that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the decision.

Inputs provide information, resources, constraints, or other factors relevant to the available alternatives.

Rules, criteria, responsibilities, and authority may define how a decision is evaluated and who may make it.

The selected action becomes part of the subsequent system process or response.

Different decisions may lead to different subsequent states, outputs, or actions.

Feedback provides information about what followed the decision.

Recording decision points, available alternatives, relevant evidence, criteria, authority, selected actions, and subsequent observations supports traceability and helps distinguish the conditions known at the time of decision from later outcomes or interpretations.

## Principle

Observe the condition.

Identify the decision point.

Record the available alternatives.

Identify the relevant evidence and criteria.

Record the decision and responsible authority.

Preserve the context.

Observe what follows.

Maintain traceability.

Review the decision when new evidence becomes available.
