System behavior describes the observable way a system changes, responds, or remains within particular conditions over time.

Behavior may appear through changes in state, outputs, flows, relationships, rates, delays, feedback, accumulation, or recurring patterns.

Identifying system behavior helps distinguish what the system is doing from assumptions about why it is doing it.

Observation records what changes.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the observed behavior.

System state provides information about conditions at a particular point in time.

Changes in inputs, constraints, dependencies, feedback, or external conditions may coincide with changes in observed behavior.

Repeated observations may reveal patterns across time.

A pattern does not by itself establish causality.

Persistence does not by itself establish that the behavior will continue.

Different system conditions may produce different behavior, even when some components remain unchanged.

Behavior may also vary across different time scales.

Recording state, timing, inputs, outputs, relationships, relevant changes, and available evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish observed behavior from interpretation.

---

## Principle

Observe what the system does.

Record how behavior changes over time.

Preserve the relevant state and context.

Separate observation from interpretation.

Do not infer causality without evidence.

Maintain traceability.

Review behavior as conditions change.
