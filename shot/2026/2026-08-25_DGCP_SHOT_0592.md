Stability describes the ability of a system, process, component, or state to remain within defined operating conditions when subject to change, disturbance, or variation.

Stability may be observed through system state, outputs, control behavior, resource conditions, response patterns, or other measurable characteristics relevant to the defined system.

Identifying stability helps distinguish normal variation from conditions in which system behavior moves outside defined operating ranges or does not return toward an expected operating condition.

Observation identifies the behavior and conditions that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding observed variation or disturbance.

System state provides information about conditions before, during, and after a change.

Inputs and events may introduce changes or disturbances.

Control flow and feedback may influence how the system responds.

Constraints and dependencies may affect the range of responses available to the system.

Stability does not mean that system state remains unchanged.

A stable system may vary over time while remaining within defined operating conditions or returning toward them after a disturbance.

Observed stability under one set of conditions does not establish stability under all conditions.

Verification helps determine whether observed behavior remains within the criteria defined for the system and conditions being examined.

Recording operating conditions, disturbances, state changes, outputs, responses, relevant evidence, and verification results supports traceability and helps distinguish observed stability from assumptions about future behavior.

---

## Principle

Define the operating conditions.

Observe the system state.

Record relevant changes or disturbances.

Observe the system response.

Compare behavior with defined criteria.

Preserve the evidence and context.

Verify what can be established.

Do not generalize beyond the observed conditions.

Maintain traceability.
