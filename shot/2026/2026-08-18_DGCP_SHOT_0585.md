Resource flow describes the movement, transfer, allocation, or transformation of resources across components, processes, or systems.

Resources may include materials, energy, information, time, capacity, infrastructure, financial resources, or other assets required for system operation.

Identifying resource flows helps clarify where resources originate, how they move, where they are transformed or consumed, and which parts of the system depend on their availability.

Observation identifies the resources, movements, and conditions that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain how and why resources move through the system.

Inputs introduce resources into a defined process or system.

Processes may use, transform, store, transfer, or distribute those resources.

Outputs represent resources, products, information, or other results leaving a process or defined system boundary.

Constraints, dependencies, capacity, timing, and interfaces may influence resource flow.

Feedback provides information about observed conditions and outcomes associated with the flow.

Recording resource sources, destinations, quantities or conditions when available, transformations, dependencies, and relevant changes supports traceability and helps distinguish observed resource movement from interpretation.

---

## Principle

Identify the resource.

Observe where it originates.

Trace how it moves.

Record where it is used, transformed, stored, or transferred.

Identify relevant constraints and dependencies.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the flow when resources, processes, dependencies, or system conditions change.
