Architecture as a system describes architecture not as a collection of isolated components, but as a structured set of elements, relationships, boundaries, interfaces, dependencies, flows, constraints, and decisions that operate together.

The behavior of an architecture emerges from how these elements interact across the whole system.

Identifying architecture as a system helps distinguish individual components from the wider structure that connects them and shapes their combined behavior.

Observation identifies the components, relationships, flows, and conditions that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain why particular architectural structures and relationships exist.

Boundaries define the scope of the architecture being examined.

Interfaces define where and how components or subsystems interact.

Dependencies identify what relies on what.

Data flow, resource flow, and control flow describe how information, resources, and actions move through the architecture.

Constraints limit available structures, behaviors, or responses.

Decision points shape which architectural paths are selected.

Failure modes, recovery paths, stability, reliability, robustness, adaptability, and integrity describe different aspects of how the architecture behaves under operating conditions and change.

No single architectural element determines the behavior of the whole system.

Changes to one component, dependency, interface, flow, boundary, or constraint may affect other connected parts of the architecture.

The effects of a change may be local, distributed, immediate, delayed, visible, or difficult to observe depending on the structure and conditions involved.

Verification helps determine whether the architecture continues to support its defined functions and relationships under the conditions being examined.

Recording components, boundaries, interfaces, dependencies, flows, constraints, decisions, state changes, evidence, and verification results supports traceability and helps preserve an understandable representation of the architecture as a system.

---

## Principle

Define the system scope.

Identify the components.

Map the boundaries and interfaces.

Trace dependencies and flows.

Identify constraints and decision points.

Observe system behavior.

Record changes and resulting states.

Preserve the evidence and context.

Verify what can be established.

Maintain traceability.

Review the architecture as relationships and conditions change.
