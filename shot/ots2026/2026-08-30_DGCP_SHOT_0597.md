Architectural trade-offs describe situations in which improving one system characteristic may affect another characteristic, constraint, capability, or operating condition.

A trade-off may involve performance, reliability, cost, complexity, flexibility, maintainability, security, interoperability, scalability, latency, resource use, or other architectural characteristics.

Identifying trade-offs helps clarify which system qualities are being prioritized, which limitations are being accepted, and which consequences may follow from a design or structural decision.

Observation identifies the existing architecture, conditions, and behavior.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the purpose, constraints, and operating conditions surrounding the decision.

Requirements define which functions or qualities the architecture is expected to support.

Constraints limit the available architectural options.

Dependencies and interfaces influence how changes in one part of the architecture may affect other parts.

Decision points identify where alternative architectural paths are available.

Each alternative may provide different benefits, limitations, dependencies, risks, and resource requirements.

A trade-off does not mean that one option is universally better than another.

The appropriate choice depends on system purpose, requirements, constraints, operating conditions, and the evidence available at the time of decision.

Architectural decisions may also create new constraints, dependencies, failure modes, or maintenance requirements that should be observed over time.

Verification helps determine whether the selected architecture performs within the criteria defined for the conditions being examined.

Recording alternatives, criteria, constraints, dependencies, expected effects, selected decisions, evidence, and subsequent observations supports traceability and helps distinguish deliberate architectural choices from unintended system consequences.

---

## Principle

Define the system purpose.

Identify the requirements.

Identify the constraints.

Record the available alternatives.

Compare relevant benefits and limitations.

Map dependencies and interfaces.

Record the selected decision and criteria.

Observe the resulting system behavior.

Preserve the evidence and context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the trade-off when requirements, constraints, evidence, or system conditions change.
