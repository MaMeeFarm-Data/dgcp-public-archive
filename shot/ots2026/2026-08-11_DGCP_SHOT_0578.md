Coupling describes the degree of dependency between connected components, processes, or systems.

Coupling may arise through shared information, resources, interfaces, timing, control, infrastructure, processes, or other system relationships.

Identifying coupling helps clarify how strongly connected parts depend on one another and how changes in one part may affect other connected parts.

Observation identifies the connection.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain how the connection operates.

Tighter coupling may increase coordination requirements and increase the effect of changes across connected parts.

Looser coupling may allow connected parts to operate with greater independence while maintaining defined interactions.

The appropriate degree of coupling depends on system purpose, structure, and operating conditions.

Feedback provides information about what followed.

Recording coupling with time, context, and relevant evidence supports traceability and helps clarify how system relationships influence behavior and change.

---

## Principle

Observe the connection.

Identify the dependency.

Assess the degree of coupling.

Record how the parts interact.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review coupling when system relationships change.
