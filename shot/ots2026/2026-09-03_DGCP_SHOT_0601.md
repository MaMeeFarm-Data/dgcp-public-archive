Dynamic relationships describe connections between system elements whose influence, strength, direction, or relevance may change over time or under different conditions.

A relationship may involve components, processes, resources, flows, dependencies, constraints, interfaces, feedback, or other system elements.

Identifying dynamic relationships helps clarify that connections within a system are not always fixed.

Observation identifies the elements and relationships that are visible.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the observed relationship.

A relationship may strengthen, weaken, appear, disappear, or change form as system conditions change.

Changes in one element may coincide with changes in another connected element.

Sequence and correlation do not by themselves establish causality.

The same relationship may behave differently under different operating conditions.

Some relationships may become visible only after repeated observation across time.

Feedback, constraints, resource availability, system state, and external conditions may influence how relationships are expressed.

Recording connected elements, timing, relevant conditions, observed changes, and available evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish observed relationships from assumptions about mechanism.

---

## Principle

Identify the connected elements.

Observe how the relationship changes.

Record the relevant conditions.

Preserve timing and context.

Separate correlation from mechanism.

Do not infer causality without evidence.

Maintain traceability.

Review the relationship as system conditions change.
