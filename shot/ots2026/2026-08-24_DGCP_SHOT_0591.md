Fault isolation is the process of identifying and separating the component, function, dependency, interface, or system area associated with an observed fault or failure condition.

Isolation may involve narrowing the affected scope, separating components, disabling interfaces, switching dependencies, comparing system states, or testing defined parts of the system.

Identifying the affected area helps distinguish where a fault is observed from where its underlying cause may exist.

Observation identifies the behavior and conditions that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the fault.

System state provides information about conditions before, during, and after the observed fault.

Failure modes describe how the failure appears.

Dependencies and interfaces help identify where effects may propagate across connected parts of the system.

Boundaries help define the scope being examined.

Isolation narrows the area of investigation. It does not by itself establish root cause.

A fault observed in one component may originate elsewhere, and multiple faults may produce similar observed behavior.

Changes made during isolation may also alter system state and should be recorded where relevant.

Verification helps determine whether the affected scope has been correctly identified and whether isolation actions produced the observed result.

Recording observations, isolation actions, affected components, dependencies, interfaces, state changes, evidence, and verification results supports traceability and subsequent investigation or recovery.

---

## Principle

Observe the fault.

Define the affected scope.

Identify relevant dependencies and interfaces.

Isolate where appropriate.

Record each isolation action.

Observe resulting state changes.

Preserve the evidence and context.

Verify the isolated scope.

Separate observed behavior from inferred cause.

Maintain traceability.
