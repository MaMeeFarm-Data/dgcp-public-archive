Abstraction is the representation of a system, process, component, or relationship by emphasizing information relevant to a defined purpose while leaving other details outside the representation.

Different abstractions may represent the same system at different levels of detail or from different perspectives.

Identifying the purpose and level of abstraction helps clarify what the representation includes, what it omits, and how it should be interpreted.

Observation identifies what exists.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps determine which details are relevant.

Abstraction organizes selected information into a representation suitable for a defined purpose.

The representation does not replace the underlying system or evidence.

Changes in purpose, context, or available evidence may require the abstraction to be reviewed or refined.

Recording abstractions with their purpose, scope, context, and relevant evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish representations from the underlying system.

---

## Principle

Observe the system.

Define the purpose.

Select the relevant detail.

Identify what is omitted.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the abstraction when purpose, context, or evidence changes.
