Cohesion describes how closely the elements within a component, process, or system relate to a shared purpose or function.

Cohesion may arise through aligned responsibilities, related functions, shared information, coordinated processes, defined boundaries, or other internal relationships.

Identifying cohesion helps clarify whether elements that operate together contribute to a coherent purpose.

Observation identifies the internal relationships.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain how the elements function together.

Higher cohesion may support clearer responsibilities, more focused functions, and easier understanding of internal structure.

Lower cohesion may indicate that unrelated responsibilities or functions have been combined within the same component or process.

The appropriate degree of cohesion depends on system purpose, structure, and operating conditions.

Feedback provides information about what followed.

Recording cohesion with time, context, and relevant evidence supports traceability and helps clarify how internal relationships influence system structure and behavior.

---

## Principle

Observe the internal relationships.

Identify the shared purpose.

Assess the degree of cohesion.

Record how the elements function together.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review cohesion when system structure or responsibilities change.
