Control flow describes the sequence and conditions that determine how actions, processes, or responses proceed within a system.

Control flow may be influenced by events, triggers, decision points, rules, system state, inputs, constraints, feedback, or other defined conditions.

Identifying control flow helps clarify what happens next, under which conditions, and how different paths through a system are selected.

Observation identifies the conditions and actions that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding each transition.

Events or triggers may initiate a process or response.

Decision points determine which available path is selected according to defined conditions, criteria, rules, or authority.

The selected path determines which subsequent action or process is performed.

System state may change as actions occur.

Feedback provides information about what followed and may inform subsequent control decisions or responses.

Control flow does not describe every resource or piece of information moving through a system. It describes how the sequence of actions and transitions is determined.

Recording triggers, conditions, decision points, selected paths, actions, state changes, and relevant evidence supports traceability and helps reconstruct how a system proceeded from one condition to another.

---

## Principle

Observe the condition.

Identify the trigger.

Identify the decision point.

Record the available paths.

Record the selected path and relevant criteria.

Observe the resulting action or state change.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the flow when rules, conditions, responsibilities, or system behavior change.
