Assumptions are conditions, beliefs, or interpretations treated as valid when complete evidence is not available.

An assumption may arise from incomplete information, prior experience, expected conditions, working models, dependencies, or other contextual factors.

Identifying assumptions helps distinguish between what is directly observed, what is supported by evidence, and what remains unverified.

Observation records what is observed.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the information.

Assumptions identify what is being treated as valid without complete verification.

New evidence may support, refine, or challenge an assumption.

Feedback provides information about what followed.

Recording assumptions with time, context, and available evidence supports traceability and helps prevent unverified interpretations from being treated as established facts.

---

## Principle

Observe what is known.

Identify what is assumed.

Separate evidence from interpretation.

Record the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review assumptions when new evidence becomes available.
