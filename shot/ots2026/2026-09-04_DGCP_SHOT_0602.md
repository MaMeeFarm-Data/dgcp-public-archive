Reinforcing loops describe feedback relationships in which a change in one part of a system is associated with further change in the same general direction.

A reinforcing loop may involve growth, decline, accumulation, depletion, adoption, withdrawal, or another process that compounds over time.

Identifying reinforcing loops helps clarify how repeated interactions may strengthen an observed direction of change.

Observation identifies the elements and changes that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the observed feedback relationship.

A change in one element may affect another element.

The resulting change may then influence the original element or another connected part of the loop.

When this relationship repeats, the magnitude of change may increase over time.

Reinforcing behavior does not mean that growth or decline will continue indefinitely.

Constraints, delays, resource limits, balancing relationships, or changing conditions may alter or interrupt the loop.

A repeated pattern does not by itself establish the underlying mechanism.

Verification and continued observation are required to distinguish an apparent reinforcing pattern from coincidence, temporary conditions, or other interacting processes.

Recording the connected elements, direction of change, timing, feedback relationships, relevant conditions, and available evidence supports traceability and helps distinguish observed reinforcement from assumptions about causality.

---

## Principle

Identify the connected elements.

Observe the direction of change.

Trace the feedback relationship.

Record repeated interactions over time.

Preserve the relevant context.

Do not assume unlimited continuation.

Do not infer mechanism without evidence.

Maintain traceability.

Review the loop as system conditions change.
