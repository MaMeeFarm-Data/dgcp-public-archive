Data flow describes the movement, transfer, transformation, storage, or exchange of data across components, processes, interfaces, or systems.

Data may originate from observations, sensors, users, transactions, processes, external systems, or other defined sources.

Identifying data flow helps clarify where data originates, how it moves, where it is transformed or stored, which interfaces it crosses, and where it is used.

Observation identifies the data, sources, movements, and conditions that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain how and why data moves through the system.

Inputs introduce data into a defined process or system.

Processes may validate, transform, combine, store, transfer, or use the data.

Interfaces define where and how data moves between components or systems.

Outputs provide data or information to subsequent processes, users, or systems.

Constraints, dependencies, permissions, formats, timing, and system state may influence data flow.

Feedback may introduce additional data that informs subsequent processes or responses.

Data flow does not by itself establish the accuracy, meaning, or reliability of the data being transferred. These require appropriate evidence, context, and verification.

Recording data sources, destinations, transformations, interfaces, relevant conditions, and available evidence supports traceability and helps reconstruct how data moved through the system.

---

## Principle

Identify the data.

Identify the source.

Trace how it moves.

Record where it is transformed, stored, or transferred.

Identify the interfaces and dependencies.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Verify where verification is required.

Review the flow when sources, processes, interfaces, permissions, or system conditions change.
