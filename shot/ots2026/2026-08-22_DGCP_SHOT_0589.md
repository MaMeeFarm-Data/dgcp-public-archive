Failure modes describe the ways in which a component, process, function, or system may fail to perform as intended under defined conditions.

A failure mode may involve loss of function, degraded performance, incorrect output, unavailable resources, interrupted flow, communication failure, dependency failure, control failure, or another deviation from expected operation.

Identifying failure modes helps clarify what can fail, how failure may appear, which conditions are associated with it, and which parts of the system may be affected.

Observation identifies the conditions and behavior that occur.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain the conditions surrounding the failure.

System state provides information about conditions before, during, or after an observed failure.

Dependencies help identify other components, resources, or systems associated with continued operation.

Constraints may influence the conditions under which failure occurs or the available responses.

A failure mode describes how failure occurs or appears. It does not by itself establish the underlying cause.

Different causes may produce similar failure modes, and a single cause may contribute to multiple failure modes.

Feedback provides information about what followed and may support subsequent investigation, response, or recovery.

Recording the observed failure mode, relevant conditions, system state, dependencies, evidence, responses, and subsequent observations supports traceability and helps distinguish observed failure behavior from inferred causes.

---

## Principle

Observe the behavior.

Identify the failure mode.

Record the relevant conditions.

Preserve the system state and evidence.

Identify affected dependencies.

Separate observed failure from inferred cause.

Record the response.

Observe what follows.

Maintain traceability.

Review the failure mode when new evidence becomes available.
