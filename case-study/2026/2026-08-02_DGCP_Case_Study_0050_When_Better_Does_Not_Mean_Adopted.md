# DGCP™ Case Study #0050

## When Better Does Not Mean Adopted

### How Observable Adoption Patterns May Differ from Observable Solution Characteristics

> *"Observable differences in solution characteristics and observable adoption patterns do not necessarily move together. Adoption may emerge through interactions among multiple observable system conditions."*

## Purpose

This case study examines how observable adoption patterns may differ from observable differences in solution characteristics.

It observes relationships among solution characteristics, user interaction patterns, familiarity, switching friction, operational conditions, behavioral transition, system context, and adoption patterns.

---

## Core Question

Why may observable adoption patterns differ despite observable differences in solution characteristics?

---

## Core Observation

Different solutions may exhibit different observable characteristics.

These differences can involve functionality, design, performance, accessibility, integration, operating requirements, interfaces, or other characteristics visible within a particular system or context.

Observable differences in solution characteristics, however, do not necessarily correspond with simultaneous differences in adoption patterns.

Existing user interaction patterns may continue over time.

Familiarity with existing tools, interfaces, processes, or operating environments may remain observable even when alternative solutions are available.

Switching friction may also be present. Transition can involve changes in routines, interfaces, processes, integrations, training, access, or other system conditions.

Behavioral transition may therefore develop gradually rather than occurring at the same time as changes in solution characteristics.

Observable adoption patterns can consequently vary across systems, user groups, operating environments, and periods of time.

   User interaction patterns may vary under similar or different operating conditions.

3. **Observable System Conditions**  
   Familiarity, switching friction, operational conditions, and contextual factors may be observed.

4. **Behavioral Transition**  
   Observable behavioral transition may occur gradually over time.

5. **Observable Adoption Pattern**  
   Adoption patterns may differ across systems, contexts, user groups, and periods.

## DGCP™ Insight

Observable adoption patterns may vary despite observable differences in solution characteristics.

A change in solution characteristics and a change in adoption behavior do not necessarily occur simultaneously.

---

## Key Observation

Observable solution improvement and observable adoption do not necessarily occur simultaneously.

The timing and pattern of adoption may differ according to the surrounding system conditions and observed interactions.

---

## DGCP™ Observation

Observable adoption patterns may emerge through interactions among multiple observable system conditions.

No single observable characteristic necessarily explains the complete adoption pattern across every system or context.

## Structural Perspective

Observable adoption patterns can involve several interconnected components:

- Observable solution characteristics
- Familiarity
- Observable switching friction
- Observable system context
- Observable behavioral transition
- Observable user interaction

These conditions may interact continuously.

Solution characteristics may differ while existing interaction patterns remain stable.

Familiarity may interact with switching friction.

System context may influence the conditions under which users interact with available solutions.

Behavioral transition may develop over time as interactions, operating conditions, and system characteristics change.

The observable relationships among these components may vary across systems and over time.

---

## Context Variation

Observable adoption patterns may vary according to:

- Observable system characteristics
- Observable user interaction patterns
- Observable operational conditions
- Observable environmental context
- Observable system maturity

The relative presence and interaction of these conditions can differ across contexts.

## Key Insight

Observable solution characteristics and observable adoption patterns represent different dimensions of a system.

Differences in one do not necessarily produce immediate or corresponding differences in the other.

Adoption patterns may develop through interactions among user behavior, familiarity, switching friction, operational conditions, environmental context, system maturity, and other observable system conditions.
