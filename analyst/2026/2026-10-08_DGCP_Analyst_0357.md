### System Context

Resource mapping remains one of the important information capabilities supporting resource-dependent systems within modern civilization.

Resource planning can depend on information.

Coordination can depend on the availability of relevant resource information.

Systems using resources can require information about resource locations, distributions, characteristics, and availability.

Civilization contains systems capable of organizing information about resources so that their recorded locations, distributions, and characteristics can become visible across relevant activities.

Many observers focus on resource locations, inventories, geographic distributions, physical assets, or other visible representations of resources.

The discussion frequently centers on where resources are located.

The structural capability through which information about resources becomes organized and accessible across systems receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across infrastructure, organizations, institutions, production systems, resource networks, and other resource-dependent systems, I found myself paying less attention to individual resource locations and more attention to the structured information connecting resource records with planning and coordination.

Resource locations remained visible.

Structured visibility became visible.

The observation was not about resource locations alone.

The observation was about resource mapping.
