### System Context

Leadership remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Organizations depend on leadership.

Institutions depend on leadership.

Collective systems depend on leadership.

Civilization depends on systems capable of establishing direction for collective activity so that people and priorities can align around coordinated action.

Many observers focus on leaders, executives, officials, commanders, founders, public figures, personalities, positions, or other visible individuals associated with leadership.

The discussion frequently centers on who the leader is.

The structural capability through which direction is established for collective activity and people and priorities become aligned receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, healthcare, education, science, technology, infrastructure, manufacturing, logistics, public administration, professional systems, organizational environments, and institutional systems, I found myself paying less attention to individual leaders and more attention to the directional capability through which collective activity becomes aligned.

Leaders remained visible.

Directional capability became visible.

The observation was not about leaders.

The observation was about leadership.
