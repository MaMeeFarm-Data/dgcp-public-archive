### System Context

Migration governance remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Political systems depend on migration governance.

Administrative systems depend on migration governance.

Cross-border systems depend on migration governance.

Civilization depends on systems capable of organizing the movement and status of people across jurisdictions so that conditions for entry, stay, and movement can remain sufficiently defined for organized cross-border mobility.

Many observers focus on migration rules, visas, permits, border controls, immigration procedures, travel documents, residency categories, or other visible mechanisms associated with migration governance.

The discussion frequently centers on individual rules governing entry or movement.

The structural capability through which the movement and status of people across jurisdictions are organized receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, public administration, transportation, labor mobility, territorial systems, cross-border movement, institutions, and other areas involving people moving between jurisdictions, I found myself paying less attention to individual migration rules and more attention to the governance structures organizing human mobility.

Migration rules remained visible.

Governed human mobility became visible.

The observation was not about migration rules.

The observation was about migration governance.
