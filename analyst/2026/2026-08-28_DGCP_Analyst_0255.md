### System Context

Narratives remain one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Societies operate through narratives.

Organizations operate through narratives.

Institutions operate through narratives.

Civilization depends on systems capable of organizing information into shared frames so that people and institutions can interpret information through sufficiently common structures of understanding.

Many observers focus on stories, explanations, accounts, histories, public messages, institutional descriptions, or other visible forms through which information is presented.

The discussion frequently centers on the stories themselves.

The structural capability through which information is organized into frames that shape collective interpretation receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, education, science, technology, economic systems, public administration, organizational systems, community structures, communication networks, institutional environments, and social relationships, I found myself paying less attention to individual stories and more attention to the narrative structures organizing information into shared frames.

Stories remained visible.

Shared frames of interpretation became visible.

The observation was not about stories.

The observation was about narratives.
