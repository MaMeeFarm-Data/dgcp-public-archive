### System Context

Shared resources remain one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Multiple participants can depend on shared resources.

Connected systems can depend on shared resources.

Continued activity can depend on continued access to shared resources.

Civilization depends on systems capable of sustaining resources used across multiple participants so that shared dependency can remain connected with potential continued access.

Many observers focus on common resources, available supplies, shared facilities, collective assets, or other visible resources used by multiple participants.

The discussion frequently centers on the resource itself.

The structural dependency created when multiple participants rely on access to the same resources receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across communities, organizations, institutions, economies, infrastructure, public systems, and other connected systems, I found myself paying less attention to individual common resources and more attention to the dependencies created through shared use.

Common resources remained visible.

Shared dependency became visible.

The observation was not about common resources.

The observation was about shared resources.
