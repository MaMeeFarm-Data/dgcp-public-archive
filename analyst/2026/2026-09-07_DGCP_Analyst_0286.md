### System Context

Role clarity remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Organizations depend on role clarity.

Institutions depend on role clarity.

Operational systems depend on role clarity.

Civilization depends on systems capable of defining responsibilities and expectations within organizational roles so that ambiguity can be reduced and coordinated performance can occur.

Many observers focus on job descriptions, titles, reporting lines, position descriptions, assignments, duties, or other visible expressions of organizational roles.

The discussion frequently centers on what a role is called or what tasks appear within its description.

The structural capability through which responsibilities and expectations become defined within organizational roles receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, healthcare, education, science, technology, infrastructure, manufacturing, logistics, public administration, professional systems, organizational environments, and institutional systems, I found myself paying less attention to job descriptions themselves and more attention to the clarity through which responsibilities and expectations are defined.

Job descriptions remained visible.

Defined responsibility became visible.

The observation was not about job descriptions.

The observation was about role clarity.
