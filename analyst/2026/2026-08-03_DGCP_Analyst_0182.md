### System Context

Ontology remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Infrastructure depends on ontology.

Organizations depend on ontology.

Institutions depend on ontology.

Civilization depends on systems capable of defining relationships between concepts so that knowledge can remain connected, meaningful, and interoperable across complex environments.

Many observers focus on concepts, categories, databases, or information models.

The discussion frequently centers on the individual elements within a system.

The structural capability allowing concepts to become connected through explicit relationships receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across infrastructure, governance, healthcare, finance, logistics, manufacturing, scientific research, public administration, cultural institutions, archives, and digital systems, I found myself paying less attention to individual concepts and more attention to the ontology systems enabling connected meaning across knowledge domains.

Concepts remained visible.

Connected meaning became visible.

The observation was not about relationships alone.

The observation was about ontology.
