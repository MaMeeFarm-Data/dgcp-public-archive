### System Context

Naming remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Infrastructure depends on naming.

Organizations depend on naming.

Institutions depend on naming.

Civilization depends on systems capable of assigning consistent names to entities so that they can be identified, recognized, distinguished, and coordinated across complex environments.

Many observers focus on labels, titles, identifiers, categories, or terminology.

The discussion frequently centers on the names themselves.

The structural capability allowing entities to acquire recognizable identities through consistent naming receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across infrastructure, governance, healthcare, finance, logistics, manufacturing, scientific research, public administration, cultural institutions, archives, and digital systems, I found myself paying less attention to individual labels and more attention to the naming systems enabling entities to remain identifiable across organizations, records, and interconnected systems.

Labels remained visible.

Identifiable entities became visible.

The observation was not about labels.

The observation was about naming.
