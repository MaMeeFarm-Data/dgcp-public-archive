### System Context

Civic participation remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Communities depend on civic participation.

Institutions interact with civic participation.

Civic systems depend on civic participation.

Civilization depends on systems capable of providing channels through which people can engage in matters affecting their communities and institutions so that public interests can potentially enter collective processes.

Many observers focus on public involvement, voting, meetings, consultations, associations, volunteering, or other visible expressions associated with civic participation.

The discussion frequently centers on individual forms of public involvement.

The structural capability through which people can participate in collective affairs affecting communities and institutions receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across communities, institutions, public systems, organizations, local affairs, and other areas involving collective processes, I found myself paying less attention to individual forms of public involvement and more attention to the participation connecting people with collective affairs.

Public involvement remained visible.

Participation in collective affairs became visible.

The observation was not about public involvement.

The observation was about civic participation.
