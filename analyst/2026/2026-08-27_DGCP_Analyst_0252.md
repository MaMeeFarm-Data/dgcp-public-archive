### System Context

Culture remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Infrastructure exists within culture.

Organizations operate within culture.

Institutions develop within culture.

Civilization depends on systems capable of carrying shared patterns of meaning across society so that collective understanding can persist across people, institutions, and generations.

Many observers focus on traditions, customs, language, symbols, rituals, practices, stories, art, or visible expressions of collective identity.

The discussion frequently centers on individual cultural practices or traditions.

The structural capability through which patterns of meaning are carried, interpreted, reproduced, and transmitted across society receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, education, science, technology, economic systems, public administration, community structures, organizational systems, communication networks, institutions, and social relationships, I found myself paying less attention to individual traditions and more attention to the cultural structures carrying shared patterns of meaning across society.

Traditions remained visible.

Shared patterns of meaning became visible.

The observation was not about traditions.

The observation was about culture.
