### System Context

Teaching remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Knowledge systems depend on teaching.

Organizations depend on teaching.

Institutions depend on teaching.

Civilization depends on systems capable of transferring knowledge and understanding between people so that accumulated knowledge can remain connected across generations.

Many observers focus on instruction, lessons, classrooms, teachers, educational materials, curricula, explanations, or other visible forms through which knowledge is presented.

The discussion frequently centers on what is taught.

The structural capability through which knowledge and understanding move between people and develop shared understanding receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across education, science, technology, healthcare, engineering, agriculture, governance, organizational systems, professional environments, institutional systems, cultural systems, and knowledge networks, I found myself paying less attention to individual acts of instruction and more attention to the teaching structures transmitting understanding between people.

Instruction remained visible.

Transmitted understanding became visible.

The observation was not about instruction.

The observation was about teaching.

Behind continuity of knowledge across generations exists teaching.

The relationship is structural.
