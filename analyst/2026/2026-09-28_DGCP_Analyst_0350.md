### System Context

Global coordination remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Countries can require coordination across borders.

Institutions interact across different systems.

Cross border systems can require alignment among distributed participants.

Civilization depends on systems capable of connecting participants across countries and institutions so that activities around shared issues can potentially become aligned.

Many observers focus on international meetings, conferences, summits, organizations, working groups, or other visible expressions associated with global coordination.

The discussion frequently centers on where international participants meet.

The structural capability through which participants across countries and institutions can align activities around shared issues receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across countries, institutions, economies, infrastructure, public systems, international organizations, and other areas involving cross border activity, I found myself paying less attention to international meetings and more attention to the coordination structures connecting distributed participants and activities.

International meetings remained visible.

Aligned action across systems became visible.

The observation was not about international meetings.

The observation was about global coordination.
