### System Context

Collective stewardship remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting shared systems within modern civilization.

Shared resources can involve stewardship across multiple participants.

Multiple participants can hold responsibilities associated with the same shared resources.

Continued resource function can depend on management and protection across participants.

Civilization depends on systems capable of connecting responsibility for shared resources across participants so that management and protection can potentially become coordinated over time.

Many observers focus on shared responsibility, collective management, protection activities, participating organizations, or other visible expressions associated with stewardship.

The discussion frequently centers on who is responsible.

The structural capability through which responsibility can extend across participants and become connected with management and protection receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across shared resources, communities, institutions, countries, international systems, and other settings involving multiple participants, I found myself paying less attention to shared responsibility itself and more attention to the structures through which responsibility can become coordinated across participants.

Shared responsibility remained visible.

Coordinated care across participants became visible.

The observation was not about shared responsibility.

The observation was about collective stewardship.
