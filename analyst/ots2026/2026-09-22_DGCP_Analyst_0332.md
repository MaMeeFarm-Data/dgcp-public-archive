### System Context

Competition remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Markets depend on competition.

Economic participants depend on competition.

Economic systems depend on competition.

Civilization depends on systems capable of allowing multiple participants to pursue overlapping market opportunities so that alternatives can remain available across economic activity.

Many observers focus on rivalry, competitors, market positions, prices, products, services, performance, or other visible expressions associated with competition.

The discussion frequently centers on who competes with whom.

The structural capability through which multiple participants can pursue overlapping market opportunities and contribute to available choices receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across markets, trade, commerce, production, services, distribution, business systems, and other areas involving economic participation, I found myself paying less attention to rivalry between individual participants and more attention to the competitive structures through which choice can remain distributed across markets.

Rivalry remained visible.

Distributed choice became visible.

The observation was not about rivalry.

The observation was about competition.

### Structural Analysis

Most people see rivalry.

Systems thinkers see competition architecture.

Competition appears as the structure through which multiple participants can pursue overlapping market opportunities so that alternatives can remain available and potentially influence patterns of demand and resource allocation across markets.

Its significance extends beyond rivalry between individual participants.

Behind multiple participants pursuing overlapping opportunities exists competition.

Behind structures supporting multiple market choices exists competition.

Behind alternatives available to demand exists competition.

Behind distributed participation in market opportunities exists competition.

Behind interactions among multiple market participants exists competition.

Behind distributed choice across markets exists competition.

The relationship is structural.

Competition provides structures through which multiple participants can pursue overlapping market opportunities.

Multiple participants can contribute to available choices.

Available choices can influence patterns of demand and resource allocation across markets.

Market allocation therefore depends partly on how participants, opportunities, and alternatives interact across economic activity.

The dependency extends across multiple systems simultaneously.
