### System Context

Discovery remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Science depends on discovery.

Knowledge systems depend on discovery.

Research environments depend on discovery.

Civilization depends on systems capable of revealing previously unknown information and relationships so that knowledge can expand and new possibilities for inquiry can emerge.

Many observers focus on findings, breakthroughs, observations, results, newly identified phenomena, or individual discoveries.

The discussion frequently centers on what was found.

The structural significance of revealing information and relationships that were previously unknown receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across science, technology, healthcare, engineering, environmental systems, astronomy, biology, social research, historical inquiry, institutional knowledge systems, and other research environments, I found myself paying less attention to individual findings and more attention to the expansion of knowledge created when previously unknown information and relationships become observable.

Findings remained visible.

Expanded knowledge became visible.

The observation was not about findings.

The observation was about discovery.
