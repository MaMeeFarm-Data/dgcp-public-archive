### System Context

Public understanding remains one of the most important yet least visible capabilities supporting modern civilization.

Public institutions depend on public understanding.

Social participation depends on public understanding.

Governance systems depend on public understanding.

Civilization depends on systems capable of connecting information with collective comprehension so that people can participate with sufficient understanding of the systems, conditions, decisions, and issues affecting society.

Many observers focus on awareness, information availability, public communication, education, media, announcements, reports, or access to information.

The discussion frequently centers on whether information reaches the public.

The structural capability through which information becomes collective comprehension and supports informed participation receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across governance, education, science, technology, healthcare, economic systems, public administration, infrastructure, community structures, communication networks, institutional environments, and social relationships, I found myself paying less attention to awareness alone and more attention to the structures connecting information with collective comprehension.

Awareness remained visible.

Shared comprehension became visible.

The observation was not about awareness.

The observation was about public understanding.
