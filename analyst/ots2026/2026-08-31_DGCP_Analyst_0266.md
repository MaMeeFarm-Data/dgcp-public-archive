### System Context

Legacy remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Knowledge systems inherit legacy.

Organizations inherit legacy.

Institutions inherit legacy.

Civilization depends on systems capable of carrying accumulated knowledge, structures, and practices across time so that foundations developed in earlier systems can remain connected with present ones.

Many observers focus on inheritance, history, traditions, institutions, monuments, records, achievements, or visible remnants of earlier generations.

The discussion frequently centers on what has been inherited.

The structural capability through which accumulated knowledge, structures, and practices remain connected across time receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across science, technology, education, infrastructure, governance, economic systems, organizational environments, institutional systems, cultural systems, knowledge systems, and social structures, I found myself paying less attention to individual inheritances and more attention to the accumulated continuity connecting past systems with present ones.

Inheritance remained visible.

Accumulated continuity became visible.

The observation was not about inheritance.

The observation was about legacy.
