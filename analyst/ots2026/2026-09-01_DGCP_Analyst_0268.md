### System Context

Inheritance remains one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Knowledge systems inherit foundations.

Organizations inherit foundations.

Institutions inherit foundations.

Civilization depends on systems capable of carrying accumulated foundations from one generation to another so that established knowledge and capabilities can remain available across time.

Many observers focus on what is received, inherited assets, traditions, records, institutions, knowledge, practices, technologies, or other visible elements passed from earlier generations.

The discussion frequently centers on the contents of inheritance.

The structural relationship through which accumulated foundations move from one generation to another and remain available for continued development receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across science, technology, education, infrastructure, governance, economic systems, organizational environments, institutional systems, cultural systems, knowledge systems, and social structures, I found myself paying less attention to individual inherited elements and more attention to the transferred foundations connecting generations.

What was received remained visible.

Transferred foundations became visible.

The observation was not about what was received.

The observation was about inheritance.
