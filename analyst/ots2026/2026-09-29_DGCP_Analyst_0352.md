### System Context

Global commons remain among the most important yet least visible shared systems supporting modern civilization.

Societies interact with global commons.

Activities across countries interact with global commons.

Shared global systems can extend beyond individual national boundaries and jurisdictions.

Civilization depends on functioning global commons that can support activities across multiple societies while extending beyond individual national systems.

Many observers focus on resources beyond borders, shared spaces, common environments, or other visible elements associated with global commons.

The discussion frequently centers on where those resources or systems are located.

The structural relationship created when shared systems extend beyond individual national systems and interact with activities across multiple societies receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across countries, institutions, societies, economies, infrastructure, and other systems operating across borders, I found myself paying less attention to individual resources beyond borders and more attention to the shared global systems connecting multiple societies.

Resources beyond borders remained visible.

Shared global systems became visible.

The observation was not about resources beyond borders.

The observation was about global commons.
