### System Context

Markets remain one of the most important yet least visible structures supporting modern civilization.

Producers depend on markets.

Consumers depend on markets.

Economic systems depend on markets.

Civilization depends on systems capable of providing structures through which participants can interact in organized exchange so that supply and demand can interact across economic activity.

Many observers focus on places to buy and sell, shops, marketplaces, transactions, buyers, sellers, products, services, or other visible expressions associated with markets.

The discussion frequently centers on where exchange occurs.

The structural capability through which participants can interact through systems of exchange receives less attention.

While reviewing developments across production, services, trade, commerce, distribution, business systems, economic activity, and other areas involving exchange, I found myself paying less attention to individual places where buying and selling occur and more attention to the market structures through which participants, supply, and demand can interact.

Places to buy and sell remained visible.

Organized exchange became visible.

The observation was not about places to buy and sell.

The observation was about markets.

### Structural Analysis

Most people see places to buy and sell.

Systems thinkers see market architecture.

Markets appear as structures through which participants can interact in systems of exchange so that supply can interact with demand and economic coordination can be supported across participants.

Their significance extends beyond physical marketplaces.

Behind structures connecting participants exist markets.

Behind organized exchange exist markets.

Behind potential interaction between supply and demand exist markets.

Behind structures supporting repeated economic exchange exist markets.

Behind connections among distributed economic participants exist markets.

Behind support for economic coordination through exchange exist markets.

The relationship is structural.

Markets provide structures through which participants can interact in systems of exchange.

Organized exchange can connect supply with demand.

Interaction between supply and demand can support economic coordination.

Economic coordination therefore depends partly on whether participants, supply, and demand can interact through functioning systems of exchange.

The dependency extends across multiple systems simultaneously.

Production systems depend on markets.

Service systems depend on markets.

Trade and commerce systems depend on markets.

Distribution systems interact with markets.

Broader economic systems depend on markets.

Viewed independently, these systems involve different participants, goods, services, forms of supply, forms of demand, and exchange relationships.

Viewed together, they reveal markets as exchange infrastructure providing structures through which participants, supply, and demand can interact and economic coordination can be supported.

Places to buy and sell remained visible.

Market architecture became visible.
