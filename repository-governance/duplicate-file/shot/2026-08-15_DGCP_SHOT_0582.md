Separation of concerns is the organization of a system so that distinct responsibilities, functions, or areas of interest can be considered and managed separately.

A concern may relate to data, processing, interfaces, security, governance, presentation, infrastructure, operations, or another defined responsibility within a system.

Identifying distinct concerns helps clarify which parts of a system are responsible for particular functions and where interactions between responsibilities occur.

Observation identifies the responsibilities and relationships that exist.

Evidence supports what can be verified.

Context helps explain why particular concerns are separated or connected.

Separation does not require isolation.

Distinct concerns may remain dependent on one another and interact through defined interfaces, processes, or shared conditions.

Changes within one concern may be managed with less effect on other concerns when responsibilities and interactions are clearly defined.

The appropriate separation depends on system purpose, structure, dependencies, and operating conditions.

Recording responsibilities, boundaries, interfaces, dependencies, and relevant changes supports traceability and helps distinguish individual concerns from their interactions with the wider system.

---

## Principle

Identify the concerns.

Define the responsibilities.

Separate where useful.

Define the interactions.

Preserve the context.

Maintain traceability.

Review the separation when responsibilities, dependencies, or system conditions change.
